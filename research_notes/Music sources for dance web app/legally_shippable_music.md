## 1. Creative Commons applied to the app's present tracks (CC BY 4.0 "Hot Swing"; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 "Sunday Reflection" and "Slow Your Roll")

### Takeaway
The CC BY 4.0 track is usable in any version of the app once it is credited properly inside the app (today the app shows only the track title; the credits live only in the repository README). The two CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 tracks raise three questions that neither the licence text, Creative Commons nor any court settles for this case: whether a donation button makes the use commercial, whether the tempo variants and the beat-synced animation create "Adapted Material" that must be licensed BY-NC-SA, and whether app-store packaging counts as a technological restriction. The licence itself allows the band to grant separate terms, so a short written permission from the band removes all three.

### Cited Findings

**Attribution (identical in both licences)**
- Section 3(a)(1): "If You Share the Licensed Material (including in modified form), You must: A. retain the following if it is supplied by the Licensor with the Licensed Material: i. identification of the creator(s) of the Licensed Material and any others designated to receive attribution, in any reasonable manner requested by the Licensor (including by pseudonym if designated); ii. a copyright notice; iii. a notice that refers to this Public License; iv. a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties; v. a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable; B. indicate if You modified the Licensed Material and retain an indication of any previous modifications; and C. indicate the Licensed Material is licensed under this Public License, and include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en); [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- Where it may be shown, Section 3(a)(2): "You may satisfy the conditions in Section 3(a)(1) in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context in which You Share the Licensed Material. For example, it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- Section 3(a)(3): "If requested by the Licensor, You must remove any of the information required by Section 3(a)(1)(A) to the extent reasonably practicable." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- The 4.0 list above has no "title" item. A university guide summarising CC's practice says the title "is a requirement of all CC licenses version 3.0 or earlier and is optional for 4.0", that author, source and licence are required in all versions, that you should "clearly indicate if changes were made", and "whenever possible, include links to both the original source and the license" (search extract). — [University of Ottawa library guide](https://uottawa.ca/library/copyright/what-is-copyright/how-attribute-creative-commons-licensed-content-best-practices)
- Creative Commons recommends the "TASL" pattern (Title, Author, Source, License); its wiki example for an adapted work credits the original and then labels the new work as adapted, with the new licensor's name (search extract). — [CC Certificate course 4.1](https://creativecommons.org/course/cc-cert-edu/unit-4-using-cc-licenses-and-cc-licensed-works/4-1-choosing-and-applying-a-cc-license/); [CC wiki: Recommended practices for attribution](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution)
- Section 2(a)(6), no endorsement: nothing in the licence is "permission to assert or imply that You are, or that Your use of the Licensed Material is, connected with, or sponsored, endorsed, or granted official status by, the Licensor". — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- State of the app on 2026-11-17: the track list file carries title, artist and a licence label ("CC BY 4.0", "CC BY-NC-SA" without version or link); the dance-floor screen draws only the title and BPM; the About string credits the font and the icons, not the music; the repository README credits the three tracks without links and without the licence version for the two band tracks. — [app track list](../../app/src/main/assets/dance/tracks.json); [app strings](../../app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml); [repository README](../../README.md)
- The audio files, including 18 tempo variants (-40 to +20 BPM, pitch unchanged, "mono, 96 kbps, 48 kHz, no tags") and the 5:15 loop of "Hot Swing", are committed to the public repository and shipped inside the APK attached to public releases. — [project music notes, 2026-10-16 to 10-19](../../music/README.md); [repository README](../../README.md)
- US law separately forbids intentionally removing "copyright management information" (title, author, "Terms and conditions for use of the work", links to such information) when the person knows or has reasonable grounds to know it will facilitate or conceal infringement (paraphrase with short quotes). — [17 U.S.C. § 1202, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/1202.md)

**NonCommercial: the text and Creative Commons' guidance**
- Definition, Section 1(k): "NonCommercial means not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation. For purposes of this Public License, the exchange of the Licensed Material for other material subject to Copyright and Similar Rights by digital file-sharing or similar means is NonCommercial provided there is no payment of monetary compensation in connection with the exchange." — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- Grant, Section 2(a)(1): a "worldwide, royalty-free, non-sublicensable, non-exclusive, irrevocable license" to "A. reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part, for NonCommercial purposes only; and B. produce, reproduce, and Share Adapted Material for NonCommercial purposes only." — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- Section 2(b)(3): the licensor waives royalties "To the extent possible ... whether directly or through a collecting society", but "In all other cases the Licensor expressly reserves any right to collect such royalties, including when the Licensed Material is used other than for NonCommercial purposes." — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC FAQ (search extract): the definition "does not turn on the type of user"; whether a use is commercial "will depend on the specifics of the situation and the intentions of the user"; it is "usually relatively easy to determine" but "there will always be uses that are challenging to categorize"; "CC cannot advise you on what is and is not commercial use. If you are unsure, [you should either] contact the rights holder for clarification or search for works that permit commercial uses." The FAQ page's metadata showed a last update about 553 days before 2026-11-17 (around May 2025). — [CC FAQ: Does my use violate the NonCommercial clause?](https://creativecommons.org/faq/#does-my-use-violate-the-noncommercial-clause-of-the-licenses)
- CC's interpretation page (search extract): the word "primarily" acknowledges that not every activity can be cleanly sorted into commercial and noncommercial; treating every for-profit use as automatically commercial conflicts with the licence's intent; the word "private" (before "monetary compensation") was dropped in version 4.0 without intending to change the meaning. — [CC wiki: NonCommercial interpretation](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/NonCommercial_interpretation); [CC wiki: 4.0/NonCommercial](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/4.0/NonCommercial)
- "Defining Noncommercial" study (published 14 September 2009, by Netpop Research for Creative Commons, about 2,000 completed US surveys, half creators and half users; search extract): on a scale of 1 ("definitely noncommercial") to 100 ("definitely commercial"), uses in connection with online advertising scored 84.6 (creators) and 82.6 (users); creators generally rated the uses studied as less commercial than users did; the study found "more uncertainty than clarity" for specific uses and advised against splitting NC into several licence flavours. **Adapted Material: looping, time-stretching, format changes**
- Section 1(a): "Adapted Material means material subject to Copyright and Similar Rights that is derived from or based upon the Licensed Material and in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified in a manner requiring permission under the Copyright and Similar Rights held by the Licensor. For purposes of this Public License, where the Licensed Material is a musical work, performance, or sound recording, Adapted Material is always produced where the Licensed Material is synched in timed relation with a moving image." — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en) (same wording in [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en))
- Section 2(a)(4): the licensor authorises use "in all media and formats whether now known or hereafter created, and to make technical modifications necessary to do so ... For purposes of this Public License, simply making modifications authorized by this Section 2(a)(4) never produces Adapted Material." — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC FAQ (search extract): generally "a modification rises to the level of an adaptation under copyright law when the modified work is based on the prior work but manifests sufficient new creativity to be copyrightable"; format changes are not adaptations "even if applicable law would suggest otherwise"; a collection of separate works is not an adaptation. — [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [CC Certificate course 4.4, Remixing](https://creativecommons.org/course/cc-cert-edu/unit-4-using-cc-licenses-and-cc-licensed-works/4-4-remixing-cc-licensed-work/)
- Under US law the sound-recording owner's derivative-work right covers works in which the actual sounds are "rearranged, remixed, or otherwise altered in sequence or quality". — [17 U.S.C. § 114(b), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/114.md)
- Under UK law, "adaptation" of a musical work "means an arrangement or transcription of the work" (section 21(3)(b), version dated 1998-01-01). — [CDPA 1988 s.21, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/21.md)

**Technological measures and app stores**
- Section 2(a)(5)(C): "No downstream restrictions. You may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, the Licensed Material if doing so restricts exercise of the Licensed Rights by any recipient of the Licensed Material." Section 1(e) defines Effective Technological Measures as "those measures that, in the absence of proper authority, may not be circumvented under laws fulfilling obligations under Article 11 of the WIPO Copyright Treaty". — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)

### Inferences
- **Attribution fix (both licences, needed now).** The APK is "Shared" without the items of Section 3(a)(1): no creator, no licence notice or link, no source link, no change notice inside the app. A credits screen (or one visible link to a credits page) satisfies Section 3(a)(2). Suggested content per track: title; creator as the licensor names it; source link; licence name with version and link; change notice. For example: *"Hot Swing" by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), CC BY 4.0 (creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Changes: looped and extended to 5:15; slower and faster versions made for this app.* And: *"Sunday Reflection" and "Slow Your Roll" by Laughing Manny & The Swinging Dicks, from "Swinging Without Words" (Free Music Archive link), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). Changes: slower and faster versions made for this app, offered under the same licence.* Because the variants were written "no tags", the credits screen and README are the only place the notices now live. If the gap is closed within 30 days of noticing it, Section 6(b) reinstates the licence automatically.
- **Donation button: probably NonCommercial, not safely so.** For: the app is free, has no ads and no paywall; the donation is optional and is not a price for the music; the test is the primary purpose of the use; CC's 2006 draft put tip jars on the noncommercial side; the app's own text frames the money as cost recovery. Against: money reaches the developer through the product that contains the music; in the 2009 survey, uses tied to money (advertising in the figures verified here) were rated clearly commercial by creators and users alike; the definition leaves the call to a court; if the donation is ever taken through a store's in-app purchase system it looks like a sale. Creative Commons' own advice for unclear cases is to ask the rights holder.
- **A store listing on a paid developer account** is not, by the wording of Section 1(k), commercial use: the fee is paid by the developer, not received. The exposure a store adds is different: the technological-measures clause (unsettled for Android packages, more doubtful for platforms that encrypt every app), the store's own rules on donations, and catalogues such as F-Droid that flag NC assets.
- **Tempo variants and the loop are best treated as Adapted Material.** They are not format conversions (Section 2(a)(4) covers only modifications needed to use another medium or format), and US law reserves to the recording owner works in which the sounds are "rearranged ... or otherwise altered in sequence or quality". Consequences: for "Hot Swing", only a change notice; for the two band tracks, the variants must themselves be offered under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (or later), with that stated and linked, and with no extra terms.
- **Beat-synced animation: unresolved, with a conservative reading that it is covered.** The clause has no fixation requirement, the animation is a moving image, and the app is built to play each recording in timed relation with it (per-track beat grids). The counter-reading is that "synching" means the film/video synchronisation of industry practice (a fixed audiovisual work), and that an app is a collection of separate works. If the clause applies: nothing changes for the CC BY track (adaptation is allowed, attribution only); for the BY-NC-SA tracks, the developer's "contributions to Adapted Material" would need a BY-NC-SA licence, and it is unclear whether that means only the synchronised output and its timing data or more of the app. For NoDerivatives music (most Jamendo swing) the clause, if it applies, rules the track out entirely, in addition to the ban on tempo changes.
- **Screen recordings or promotional videos of the app with a BY-NC-SA track playing are Adapted Material without any doubt** (music fixed in timed relation with a moving image): such a video must be BY-NC-SA 4.0 and non-commercial (no monetised upload).
- **What a separate permission usually contains** (inference from the clauses above and common practice): the tracks by title; who grants (and that they control both recording and composition); the uses allowed (inclusion in a free app for Android, iOS and the web; edited, looped and tempo-changed versions; playback with animation, metronome and voice; distribution through app stores and a public code repository; optional donations; promotional videos); territory worldwide; term perpetual or fixed with renewal; credit wording; fee or "free of charge"; what happens if the app later charges or shows ads; signature or a clear email reply. An email exchange is enough to be an "expressly agreed" separate arrangement under Section 7.
- The CC licences are irrevocable for copies already obtained, so a dated record of the Free Music Archive page showing the licence is worth keeping.

## 2. Public-domain sound recordings, and the separate copyright in the compositions

### Takeaway
Read from the statutes: in the United States a recording first published in 1923–1946 is protected for 100 years, so swing-era sides of 1935–1945 become free on 1 January 2036 to 2046; in the EU and UK the 70-year term applies only to recordings still protected on 1 November 2013, so every recording published in 1962 or earlier is free of producers' and performers' rights. The compositions are a second layer (life of the last author plus 70 years in the EU, UK and Germany; 95 years from publication for pre-1978 US works), so a swing-era recording is never free in all the target countries at once before the late 2030s; only pre-1926 recordings (pre-1927 from 1 January 2027) of long-dead composers are free everywhere today, and those are not swing-era music. Israel's term could not be verified in this session.

### Cited Findings

**United States: pre-1972 sound recordings (17 U.S.C. § 1401)**
- Term (paraphrase of the mirrored text, with its short quotes): the prohibition on unauthorised use applies "through December 31 of the year" that is 95 years after first publication, plus a transition period: for recordings first published before 1923 the transition ends on 31 December of the year three years after enactment; for recordings first published 1923–1946 it ends five years after the 95-year period; for 1947–1956, fifteen years after it; for all other recordings fixed before 15 February 1972 it ends on 15 February 2067; and in no case does protection continue after 15 February 2067. — [17 U.S.C. § 1401(a)(2), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/1401.md)
- Remedies are those of copyright infringement (sections 502–505 and 1203). — [17 U.S.C. § 1401(a)(1), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/1401.md)
- Noncommercial-use exception, § 1401(c) (paraphrase): a noncommercial use of a pre-1972 recording that is not being commercially exploited does not violate the section if the user first makes a good-faith, reasonable search (Copyright Office schedules and services offering a comprehensive set of recordings for sale or streaming) and finds nothing, files a notice of the use with the Copyright Office, and the rights owner does not opt out within 90 days of the notice being indexed. Rules of construction: "merely recovering costs of production and distribution of a sound recording" from a permitted use does not by itself make it commercial, and the user's other commercial activities do not either. — [17 U.S.C. § 1401(c), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/1401.md)
- Fair use and the library exceptions (sections 107, 108, 109, 110, 112(f)) apply to these claims, and "Section 512 shall apply to a claim under subsection (a) with respect to a sound recording fixed before February 15, 1972" (the notice-and-takedown safe harbour). — [17 U.S.C. § 1401(f), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/1401.md)

**United States: compositions**
- Works created from 1978: "the life of the author and 70 years after the author's death"; joint works: 70 years after the last surviving author's death; anonymous works and works made for hire: "95 years from the year of its first publication, or a term of 120 years from the year of its creation, whichever expires first". — [17 U.S.C. § 302, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/302.md)
- Older works: a first term of 28 years "from the date it was originally secured" and a renewal term of 67 years; a copyright in its renewal term when the 1998 extension took effect "shall have a copyright term of 95 years from the date copyright was originally secured." — [17 U.S.C. § 304, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/304.md)
- "All terms of copyright provided by sections 302 through 304 run to the end of the calendar year in which they would otherwise expire." — [17 U.S.C. § 305, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/305.md)

**European Union (Directive 2006/116/EC as amended by Directive 2011/77/EU)**
- Producers, Article 3(2): "The rights of producers of phonograms shall expire 50 years after the fixation is made. However, if the phonogram has been lawfully published within this period, the said rights shall expire 70 years from the date of the first lawful publication. If no lawful publication has taken place within the period mentioned in the first sentence, and if the phonogram has been lawfully communicated to the public within this period, the said rights shall expire 70 years from the date of the first lawful communication to the public." The paragraph does not protect anew phonograms that were no longer protected on 22 December 2002 under the earlier rule. — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 3, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-003.md)
- Performers, Article 3(1): 50 years after the performance; if a fixation "in a phonogram is lawfully published or lawfully communicated to the public within this period, the rights shall expire 70 years from the date of the first such publication or the first such communication to the public, whichever is the earlier." — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 3, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-003.md)
- Which recordings got 70 years, Article 10(5): the amended Article 3 "shall apply to fixations of performances and phonograms in regard to which the performer and the phonogram producer are still protected, by virtue of those provisions in the version thereof in force on 30 October 2011, as at 1 November 2013 and to fixations of performances and phonograms which come into being after that date." — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 10, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-010.md)
- Authors, Article 1: rights "shall run for the life of the author and for 70 years after his death"; for joint works the term is "calculated from the death of the last surviving author"; for a musical composition with words it "shall expire 70 years after the death of the last of the following persons to survive" (lyricist and composer), provided "both contributions were specifically created for the respective musical composition with words". Article 10(6) applies that rule to compositions of which music or lyrics were protected in at least one Member State on 1 November 2013. — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 1, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-001.md); [Art. 10](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-010.md)
- Counting, Article 8: "The terms laid down in this Directive shall be calculated from the first day of January of the year following the event which gives rise to them." — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 8, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-008.md)
- Non-EU works and rightholders, Article 7 (paraphrase): where the country of origin is a third country and the author is not a Community national, protection ends when it ends in the country of origin and may not exceed the Article 1 term; for related-rights holders who are not Community nationals the term must "expire no later than the date of expiry" in the rightholder's own country and may not exceed Article 3, "without prejudice to the international obligations of the Member States". — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 7, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-007.md)
- German implementation: "Das Urheberrecht erlischt siebzig Jahre nach dem Tode des Urhebers" (§ 64); joint authors: seventy years after the death of the longest-living co-author (§ 65(1)); "Die Schutzdauer einer Musikkomposition mit Text erlischt 70 Jahre nach dem Tod des Längstlebenden" (§ 65(3)); terms start at the end of the calendar year (§ 69); producer's right: "Das Recht erlischt 70 Jahre nach dem Erscheinen des Tonträgers" (§ 85(3)); the longer terms apply to recordings whose protection "am 1. November 2013 ... noch nicht erloschen war" (§ 137m(1)). The mirror's file is marked as last amended in 2020–2021. — [UrhG, German federal law mirror](https://github.com/bundestag/gesetze/blob/master/u/urhg/index.md)

**United Kingdom (Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988)**
- Sound recordings, section 13A(2) (version dated 2020-12-31, "In force"): copyright expires "at the end of the period of 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which the recording is made", or "if during that period the recording is published, 70 years from the end of the calendar year" of first publication, or, if not published but "made available to the public by being played in public or communicated to the public", 70 years from that; where the author is not a UK national, "the duration of copyright is that to which the sound recording is entitled in the country of which the author is a national", "provided that does not exceed the period which would apply under subsection (2)". — [CDPA 1988 s.13A, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/13A.md)
- Compositions, section 12 (version dated 2020-12-31): "Copyright expires at the end of the period of 70 years from the end of the calendar year in which the author dies"; joint works run from "the death of the last of them to die"; and section 12(6): "Where the country of origin of the work is not the United Kingdom" and "the author of the work is not a national of the United Kingdom," "the duration of copyright is that to which the work is entitled in the country of origin," "provided that does not exceed the period which would apply under subsections (2) to (5)." — [CDPA 1988 s.12, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/12.md)
- "Country of origin", section 15A (version dated 2020-12-31): a work first published in a Berne Convention country and not simultaneously elsewhere originates there; if first published simultaneously in several countries "only one of which is a Berne Convention country", that country; if in several Berne countries not including the UK, "the Berne Convention country which grants the shorter or shortest period of copyright protection"; "simultaneous" means "publication within 30 days of first publication". — [CDPA 1988 s.15A, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/15A.md)

**Which law a worldwide app answers to**
- EU conflict rule: "The law applicable to a non-contractual obligation arising from an infringement of an intellectual property right shall be the law of the country for which protection is claimed", and the parties cannot choose another law by agreement. — [Regulation (EC) No 864/2007 (Rome II) Art. 8, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/rome-ii/art-008.md)
- On-demand delivery is a restricted act for all three kinds of rightholder in the EU: authors have the exclusive right of "communication to the public", including "the making available to the public of their works"; performers and phonogram producers have the right of making available "in such a way that members of the public may access them from a place and at a time individually chosen by them". — [Directive 2001/29/EC Art. 3, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/infosoc-directive/art-003.md)

**New transfers, restorations, government recordings**
- UK: "Copyright does not subsist in a sound recording which is, or to the extent that it is, a copy taken from a previous sound recording." (section 5A(2), version dated 1996-01-01). — [CDPA 1988 s.5A, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/5A.md)
- Germany: the producer's right belongs to the maker of the recording, and "Das Recht entsteht nicht durch Vervielfältigung eines Tonträgers." (§ 85(1)). — [UrhG § 85, German federal law mirror](https://github.com/bundestag/gesetze/blob/master/u/urhg/index.md)
- US: a derivative work needs "modifications which, as a whole, represent an original work of authorship". — [17 U.S.C. § 101, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/101.md)
- US: "Copyright protection under this title is not available for any work of the United States Government". — [17 U.S.C. § 105(a), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/105.md)
- The project's sweep found a live 18-piece big-band track by a US military band on the Free Music Archive under the Public Domain Mark, composer uncredited ("US Army Blues, Walk That Dog"). — [project music notes, 2026-10-16](../../music/README.md)

### Inferences
- **US recordings, by year of first publication (computed from § 1401(a)(2)):** before 1923: free since 1 January 2022 (the Act dates from October 2018, see Gaps); 1923–1946: free on 1 January of (year + 101), so 1925 → 1 Jan 2026 (already free), 1926 → 1 Jan 2027, 1930 → 2031, 1935 → 2036, 1936 → 2037, 1937 → 2038, 1938 → 2039, 1939 → 2040, 1940 → 2041, 1941 → 2042, 1942 → 2043, 1943 → 2044, 1944 → 2045, 1945 → 2046, 1946 → 2047; 1947–1956: free on 1 January of (year + 111), so 1947 → 2058 and 1956 → 2067; 1957 to 14 February 1972, and recordings never published (airchecks, unissued takes): 15 February 2067. The clock runs from publication, not from the recording date.
- **US compositions published before 1978** (assuming the formalities were met): free on 1 January of (year + 96): 1930 → 1 Jan 2026 (already), 1931 → 2027, 1932 → 2028, 1935 → 2031, 1936 → 2032, 1937 → 2033, 1938 → 2034, 1939 → 2035, 1940 → 2036, 1941 → 2037, 1945 → 2041.
- **EU and UK recordings:** a recording published in 1962 or earlier had 50 years from publication, which ended on or before 31 December 2012, before the cut-off of 1 November 2013: free. A recording published in 1963 or later is protected for 70 years (1963 → free 1 January 2034). All Basie, Goodman, Webb, Lunceford and Miller sides issued in 1935–1945 are therefore free as recordings in the EU and UK, and have been for decades. One trap: studio material recorded in the swing era but first published in 1963 or later (alternate takes, vault issues) took its term from that publication and can still be protected (for example first issued in 1975 → 31 December 2045).
- **A worldwide app must satisfy every country it reaches.** Under the "country for which protection is claimed" rule each user's country applies its own law; the US does not shorten its terms because a recording is free abroad, and section 1401(f)(3) gives rights owners the takedown route against a US host or store. The practical choices are the lowest common denominator (free in the US and in life-plus-70 countries) or restricting distribution by country.
- **Comparison of terms may shorten protection of US works in the UK and parts of the EU** (UK section 12(6), Directive Article 7(1)): a US-origin composition already free in the US would then be free there too. This is unreliable in practice: the outcome depends on where the work counts as first published (simultaneous publication within 30 days in another Berne country changes the country of origin) and on "international obligations" such as bilateral treaties. It should not be relied on for a specific tune without checking that tune.
- **A plain transfer or clean-up of an old disc gets no new right** in the UK or Germany (copy of a previous recording), and in the US only original authorship is protected, which mechanical processing lacks; a reissue label's remaster of a free recording is therefore usable in principle, subject to the site's contract terms and to the US position on the underlying recording.
- **US military-band recordings** are free of US copyright as recordings, but that says nothing about the compositions and arrangements they play, nor about copyright outside the US.

## 4. Direct licences from independent swing bands and labels

### Takeaway
A direct licence is the route that fits this app best, because one small rights holder can grant everything the app needs (bundling, tempo changes, synced animation, stores, donations) in one page; the catch is the composition: the band can only grant what it controls, and the US compulsory licence for cover versions does not reach audio that accompanies visuals or that is altered. No published fee data for app licences from swing bands was found.

### Cited Findings
- Two separate US rights are involved in any recording: the sound recording (rights of reproduction, derivative works, distribution and digital audio transmission only; no general public-performance right) and the musical work. — [17 U.S.C. § 114(a)–(b), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/114.md)
- US compulsory ("mechanical") licence for a composition already released: available to "make and distribute phonorecords" when "the primary purpose in making phonorecords of the musical work is to distribute them to the public for private use", "including by means of digital phonorecord delivery"; it includes "the privilege of making a musical arrangement of the work to the extent necessary to conform it to the style or manner of interpretation of the performance involved, but the arrangement shall not change the basic melody or fundamental character of the work". — [17 U.S.C. § 115(a), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- That licence does not cover music tied to visuals: "Phonorecords" are objects in which sounds "other than those accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work" are fixed, and "A digital phonorecord delivery does not include the digital transmission of sounds accompanying a motion picture or other audiovisual work as defined in section 101"; audiovisual works are "a series of related images which are intrinsically intended to be shown by the use of machines ... together with accompanying sounds, if any". — [17 U.S.C. § 101](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/101.md); [17 U.S.C. § 115(e)(10), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- A download and an on-demand stream are both "digital phonorecord deliveries" in US law ("includes a permanent download, a limited download, or an interactive stream"); to transmit a performance "to the public ... at the same time or at different times" is a public performance. — [17 U.S.C. § 115(e)(10)](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md); [17 U.S.C. § 101, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/101.md)
- In the EU, on-demand delivery needs permission from authors, performers and producers alike (Article 3 of Directive 2001/29/EC, quoted in section 2). — [Directive 2001/29/EC Art. 3, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/infosoc-directive/art-003.md)
- A CC licensor who belongs to a collecting society may have royalties collected for uses outside the licence (Section 2(b)(3), quoted in section 1). — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- What happens without composition licences (sibling notes): members of the National Music Publishers' Association sued Peloton in 2019 over songs used in class videos; the claim rose from US$150 million to more than US$300 million; settled 27 February 2020, terms undisclosed. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-publishers-and-peloton-reach-settlement-over-copyright-infringement-lawsuit); [sibling notes, section 6](precedents_from_similar_products.md)
- How dance schools and dance tools get music (sibling notes): studios buy blanket public-performance licences for in-person classes (PPL PRS in the UK, ASCAP/BMI/SESAC in the US), which do not cover an app; one online dance-class platform (Steezy) is reported by its vendor to use a cleared catalogue through Tuned Global; the dance-specific practice tools found either synthesise their own rhythm sounds or play the user's own files. — [PPL PRS: fitness and dance classes](https://pplprs.co.uk/themusiclicence/sectors/fitness-dance/fitness-exercise-and-dance-classes/); [sibling notes, sections 6 and 7](precedents_from_similar_products.md)
- Licensed catalogues are not permanent: karaoke and music-learning services tell users that songs disappear when rights holders change terms (sibling notes). — [sibling notes, section 6](precedents_from_similar_products.md)
- The project's own plan lists "Ask a local swing band or a session sax player for a CC BY recording of an original riff tune at 130, 145 and 160 BPM. Dancers' communities do this for practice material." — [project music notes](../../music/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Rights to clear for a track shipped inside the app or streamed from the developer's server:** (1) the master recording, from the band or its label (reproduction, distribution or making available, and the right to make altered versions); (2) performers' consents, which the band should warrant; (3) the composition: reproduction, making available or public performance, and, because the audio is played with synchronised visuals and changed in tempo, a negotiated grant from the writers or publisher: the US statutory mechanical licence does not fit either feature; (4) nothing from a collecting society if the writers control their own rights and grant them directly.
- **The simplest case is an original tune written by band members who are not tied to a publisher or to a society's exclusive mandate**: one signature covers everything. Covers of copyrighted standards are the hard case: the band cannot grant the composition, and the publisher's permission is needed.
- **A one-page licence should state:** the tracks (title, duration, an identifier); that the band owns or controls recording and composition and has the players' consent; the grant (non-exclusive; reproduce, edit, loop, change tempo without changing pitch, mix with a metronome and spoken cues, play with animation; distribute inside the app on Android, iOS and the web, including offline copies; use in screenshots and demo videos); territory (worldwide); term (perpetual, or a fixed term with renewal, and what happens to installed copies afterwards); money (free app, optional donations; what triggers renegotiation: ads, paid tiers, a download count); whether the files may sit in a public source repository (if not, they must be fetched at build time from private storage); credit wording; no endorsement; fee; governing law; signatures.
- For the present band tracks, the same letter can also waive the ShareAlike and NonCommercial conditions for this app, which makes the open questions of section 1 irrelevant.

### Gaps
- **Unverified background (researcher's memory; check at the page named):**
  - In the US a download is not a public performance (*United States v. ASCAP*, 2d Cir. 2010), so tracks bundled in an app download need no performing-rights licence there, while streaming from a web app does. High confidence.
  - US writers can license directly even if they belong to ASCAP or BMI (the societies' grants are non-exclusive under their consent decrees); many European societies take the performing and making-available rights exclusively (PRS by assignment, GEMA by its membership agreement, with a non-commercial exception introduced later), so a European band whose writers are members may be unable to grant the composition rights for online use by itself. Medium-high confidence; this is the point to ask any band first.
  - The US statutory mechanical rate for a download of a song up to five minutes was 12.4 cents in 2024 and 12.7 cents in 2025, adjusted yearly for inflation (about 13 cents in 2026, not confirmed); small quantities are licensed through services such as Harry Fox Agency's Songfile or Easy Song for a per-song fee of roughly US$15–17 plus royalties. Medium confidence. Check [37 CFR Part 385](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-III/subchapter-E/part-385) and [harryfox.com](https://www.harryfox.com/).
  - Typical fees for a direct app licence from an independent band: I know of no published benchmark. My impression is that such deals range from free-with-credit to a few hundred dollars per track for a perpetual non-exclusive licence. Low confidence; not a sourced figure.
- No example was found of a swing band's published licence terms for apps or instructional products, and no example of a Lindy Hop app that licensed band recordings.
- Not researched: Israeli practice (ACUM membership terms and whether members can license their own works directly).

## 6. Business-to-business "music for apps" services

### Takeaway
These services exist to put famous recordings into funded products, by quote, and the two whose developer material could be read confirm the mismatch with this app: Feed.fm's web SDK plays "DMCA compliant radio" (play, pause, skip, volume; no song choice, no access to the audio, not supported on mobile web, region-limited), and Tuned Global's delivery API requires a partner account. None of them gives a developer the audio file to time-stretch, analyse or mix under a metronome.

### Cited Findings
- Feed.fm's JavaScript SDK "allows you to play DMCA compliant radio within your browser"; controls are `play()`, `pause()`, `stop()`, `skip()` and volume; a `skip-denied` event fires when "the server denied it (due to skip restrctions)" [sic]; the `music-unavailable` event means the client is not "located in a region for which music is licensed for playback"; "we do NOT support mobile web browsers using our Javascript SDK"; production use needs "a set of production credentials from your contact at Feed.fm". The npm package was last modified on 2024-05-14 (version 1.103.6). — [Feed.fm Javascript-SDK README](https://github.com/feedfm/Javascript-SDK); [feed-media-audio-player on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/feed-media-audio-player)
- Feed.fm publishes SDKs for iOS (updated 19 Oct 2026), Android (25 Jun 2026), JavaScript (14 May 2024) and React Native (16 Apr 2024); its iOS SDK "allows you to add popular music to your mobile app". — [Feed.fm on GitHub](https://github.com/feedfm); [Feed.fm iOS-SDK README](https://github.com/feedfm/iOS-SDK)
- Feed.fm pricing (sibling notes): "a monthly subscription service with fees based on variables including: level of curation customization, how many song streams you plan to do in a month, and what use case you need covered"; by request; an aggregator lists "from $250" (unverified there). — [Feed.fm pricing page](https://www.feed.fm/pricing-page); [sibling notes, section 6](precedents_from_similar_products.md)
- The US statutory definitions behind "radio-style" services: an "interactive service" is one that lets a member of the public receive a program made specially for them or a particular recording they request (paraphrase); the "sound recording performance complement" for non-interactive channels is, in any three-hour period, no more than three selections from one album (no more than two consecutively) or four by one featured artist (no more than three consecutively) (paraphrase). — [17 U.S.C. § 114(j), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/114.md)
- Tuned Global's Delivery API (MCP server published April 2026) requires "A TunedGlobal partner account with Delivery API credentials" and offers catalogue search, "a signed stream URL for a track (AAC, MP3, HLS, DASH)", preview clips, a "download shelf", a content allowlist and play logging. — [@tuned-global/content-delivery-mcp on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@tuned-global/content-delivery-mcp)
- Epidemic Sound has a "Partner Content API"; its public iOS demo plays HLS audio streams accessed with signed cookies. — [epidemicsound/partner-content-api-demo-ios](https://github.com/epidemicsound/partner-content-api-demo-ios)
- 7digital and Songtradr publish API clients on npm (7digital's last updated 2022; a Songtradr client superseding the earlier Musicube one). — [7digital-api on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/7digital-api); [@musicube/api-client on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@musicube/api-client)
- Mubert offers an API that "generates royalty-free music from a text prompt, a genre or mood channel, or an image — at the exact length you ask for", for "videos ..., apps, games and podcasts", with optional BPM and key for channels, loop mode, MP3 or WAV; a trial key gives "a few free tracks, valid for 7 days" (package dated 30 October 2026). This is generated music, not licensed popular music. — [@mubert/n8n-nodes-mubert on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@mubert/n8n-nodes-mubert)
- Publishers name fitness, social and gaming start-ups as the main applicants for licences; one online dance-class platform is reported to source full commercial tracks through Tuned Global; a VR fitness product built on licensed hits lost its owner's support in 2026 and relaunched at about US$20 a month with only as much of the library as licensing allows (sibling notes). — [sibling notes, section 6](precedents_from_similar_products.md)

### Inferences
- The app's three needs (tempo change, beat-accurate timing against the developer's own beat grid, and the app's own sounds over the music) all require the audio itself. A radio-style SDK gives none of them, and a catalogue API that returns stream URLs gives the audio only under a commercial contract that would regulate alteration.
- Even where a web SDK exists (Feed.fm), it is desktop-only in official support, which rules out a phone web app.
- For one developer with a free app there is no entry-level tier: every verified source says "contact" or "partner account".

  - Songtradr bought 7digital in 2023 and offers catalogue APIs with licensing consultancy; a client must still hold label and publisher licences for on-demand use; minimum commitments are in the tens of thousands of dollars a year. Low-medium confidence.

## 7. Collecting-society and label licences for interactive use in an app or on a website

### Takeaway
Societies license compositions, not the recordings of famous bands: for on-demand use the recording must come from the label by direct deal in every country examined, which alone makes this route impractical for one developer. The statutory texts confirm the structure (US blanket mechanical licence only for a "digital music provider" that reports revenue and usage and pays an administrative assessment; exclusive making-available rights for authors, performers and producers in the EU); the fee schedules and small-website schemes could not be checked in this session.

### Cited Findings
- US mechanical rights for streaming and downloads: "A digital music provider that qualifies for a compulsory license under subsection (a) may, by complying with the terms and conditions of this subsection, obtain a blanket license from copyright owners through the mechanical licensing collective to make and distribute digital phonorecord deliveries of musical works through one or more covered activities." — [17 U.S.C. § 115(d)(1), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- A "digital music provider" must have "a direct contractual, subscription, or other economic relationship with end users" (or direct control of the service), be "able to fully report on any revenues and consideration generated by the service" and "able to fully report on usage of sound recordings of musical works by the service"; the Act also defines an "administrative assessment" as "the fee established pursuant to subsection (d)(7)(D)" (who pays it and how much was not read). — [17 U.S.C. § 115(e)(3), (8), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- "Covered activity" means making a digital phonorecord delivery "including in the form of a permanent download, limited download, or interactive stream"; an "interactive stream" is one that does not qualify for the statutory webcasting licence. Sounds accompanying an audiovisual work are outside the definition (section 4). — [17 U.S.C. § 115(e)(7), (10), (13), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- US sound recordings: the owner's rights include digital audio transmission, and a service that plays a particular recording on request is "interactive" (paraphrase), which puts it outside the statutory licence described in section 6. — [17 U.S.C. § 114, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/114.md)
- EU: authors, performers and phonogram producers each hold the exclusive making-available right, and it is not exhausted by any act of communication. — [Directive 2001/29/EC Art. 3, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/infosoc-directive/art-003.md)
- Blanket licences that dance studios buy for classes do not cover an app; Feed.fm states that ordinary performing-rights licences "do not include the right to use the songs in fitness classes" (sibling notes). — [Feed.fm music licensing 101](https://www.feed.fm/music-licensing-101); [sibling notes, section 6](precedents_from_similar_products.md)
- Where the recording is out of protection, only the composition layer remains: in the EU and UK every recording published by 1962 is free of producers' and performers' rights (section 2). — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 10, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-010.md)

### Inferences
- **What an on-demand service with commercial recordings would need:** in the US, performing-rights licences from each society for streamed compositions, the mechanical blanket licence with monthly reporting, and a direct licence from each label; in the UK and EU, a society licence for the composition (performing and mechanical together) per territory or through a multi-territory hub, and again a direct licence from each label; in Israel, the authors' society and the recording-rights bodies. No society anywhere licenses tempo-altered versions or synchronisation with visuals; those are for the publisher and label.
- **Why it is impractical for one developer:** the labels (no collective exists for on-demand use), the per-country multiplication, the reporting duties, and minimum fees without revenue to set against them.
- **The one combination that is workable on paper:** out-of-protection recordings (published by 1962) plus a small-service composition licence from the local society would legalise, for example, a 1938 Basie side in a UK-only web app. It fails for a worldwide app because the same recording is protected in the US until 2039 and each further country needs its own society licence; and whether a society licence extends to tempo-changed playback with synced animation is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- **Unverified background (researcher's memory; check at the page named):**
  - US performing rights for a small website or app: ASCAP, BMI and SESAC each sell a website/app licence with a minimum fee of a few hundred dollars a year; GMR negotiates individually. Low-medium confidence on amounts. Check [ascap.com](https://www.ascap.com/music-users), [bmi.com/licensing](https://www.bmi.com/licensing), [sesac.com](https://www.sesac.com/).
  - The Mechanical Licensing Collective has administered the blanket licence since 1 January 2021; rates are in 37 CFR Part 385 (headline rate rising to 15.35% of service revenue by 2027, with other prongs); licensees pay a share of the administrative assessment with an annual minimum I recall as US$5,000 or US$60,000 depending on scale. Medium confidence. Check [themlc.com](https://www.themlc.com/) and [37 CFR Part 390](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-III/subchapter-E/part-390).
  - UK: PRS for Music's Limited Online Music Licence covers composition rights (performing and mechanical) for small UK services with annual revenue under £12,500, in bands by usage starting at roughly £150 plus VAT a year; PPL does not license on-demand use of recordings for consumer apps. Medium confidence on the structure, low on the price. Check [PRS: Limited Online Music Licence](https://www.prsformusic.com/licences/using-music-online/limited-online-music-licence).
  - Germany: GEMA has on-demand tariffs with minimum fees and an online shop for small website uses; GVL licenses recordings only for webcasting-type uses. Other EU societies (SACEM, Buma/Stemra) and APRA AMCOS in Australia have comparable small-site schemes. Medium-low confidence; no figures remembered.
  - Multi-territory composition licensing for online services runs through hubs such as ICE under Directive 2014/26/EU and is built for large services. Medium confidence.
  - Israel: ACUM is the authors' and publishers' society; recording rights are administered by two producers' federations (the Israeli federation affiliated to IFPI and a federation for Israeli and Mediterranean music) and performers' rights by EILAM and Eshkolot. Medium confidence on names, none on tariffs. Check [acum.org.il](https://www.acum.org.il/).
- Not found: any 2026 tariff, any confirmation of a micro-licence that covers an app (rather than a website), and any society statement on altered-tempo playback.

## 8. Commissioning recordings

### Takeaway
Commissioning is the only route that delivers real-band swing at exactly the chosen tempos with no downstream conditions, and the statutes show how to keep it clean: use tunes that are out of copyright in every target country or newly written ones, and take a written transfer or broad licence from every writer, arranger and player. No cost figures or dance-community examples could be verified in this session.

### Cited Findings
- New compositions and new arrangements are protected: a "derivative work" includes a "musical arrangement"; new works last for the author's life plus 70 years, and a "work made for hire" 95 years from publication or 120 from creation. — [17 U.S.C. § 101](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/101.md); [17 U.S.C. § 302, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/302.md)
- Recording a copyrighted standard under the US compulsory licence would not give the app what it needs (no visuals, no change of "basic melody or fundamental character", and an arrangement made under it is not protected "except with the express consent of the copyright owner"). — [17 U.S.C. § 115(a)(2), U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/115.md)
- Tunes free in life-plus-70 countries are those whose last surviving writer died more than 70 years ago (EU Article 1, UK section 12, German § 64–65, quoted in section 2). — [Directive 2006/116/EC Art. 1, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/copyright-term-directive/art-001.md)
- In the UK the author of a sound recording is "the producer" (section 9(2)(aa), version dated 2003-10-31). — [CDPA 1988 s.9, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/9.md)
- In the US a commissioned work is a "work made for hire" only if it is "specially ordered or commissioned for use as a contribution to a collective work, as a part of a motion picture or other audiovisual work, as a translation, as a supplementary work, as a compilation, as an instructional text, as a test, as answer material for a test, or as an atlas", and only "if the parties expressly agree in a written instrument signed by them that the work shall be considered a work made for hire"; otherwise only "a work prepared by an employee within the scope of his or her employment" qualifies. A stand-alone sound recording is not on the list. — [17 U.S.C. § 101, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/101.md)
- The project's own list of realistic paths includes commissioning a band or session player for recordings "at 130, 145 and 160 BPM", and "Generate a bed and overdub a real sax player". — [project music notes](../../music/README.md)
- Precedent for making one's own sound instead of licensing recordings: a salsa practice app builds its music from switchable instrument tracks and an instructor voice at any tempo (sibling notes). — [sibling notes, section 7](precedents_from_similar_products.md)

### Inferences
- **Choice of material.** Free in the US and in life-plus-70 countries alike (inputs from memory, section 2): "King Porter Stomp", "Rose Room", "Indiana", "Avalon", "Charleston" (from 2026), ragtime, traditional tunes. New riff tunes on a blues or "rhythm changes" form avoid the question entirely, since chord progressions and forms are not protected as such (general principle, not sourced here).
- **Paper trail.** One agreement per contributor (composer of any new tune, arranger, each player, engineer if creative): assignment or exclusive licence of all rights in the composition, arrangement, performance and recording, worldwide and for the full term, with consent to editing and tempo changes; a statement of collecting-society membership, because a society member in Europe may not be free to transfer online rights in a new composition (section 4); payment as a one-off fee.
- **Freedom afterwards.** Owning the masters lets the developer publish them under CC BY or CC0, commit them to the public repository, ship them in any store and render any tempo, and gives the swing-dance community reusable practice tracks.
- **Recording for tempo.** Recording each tune at two or three tempos is musically better than stretching one take by 30%; a click track fixes the beat grid in advance, which removes the hand annotation the project needed for the live band track.
- **Rough order of cost (researcher's estimate, not sourced):** a remote small group (rhythm section and one or two horns) is likely to cost a few hundred to low thousands of dollars per tune including mixing; a studio day with a full big band runs to five figures.

### Gaps
- **Unverified background (researcher's memory; check at the page named):**
  - Union session scales: about US$450–480 per player for a three-hour US session under the main recording agreement (leader double, lower "low budget" scales exist); in the UK about £130 for a three-hour session under the Musicians' Union/BPI agreement. Low-medium confidence. Check [musiciansunion.org.uk](https://musiciansunion.org.uk/) and the American Federation of Musicians' scale summaries.
  - Online session marketplaces (SoundBetter, AirGigs, Fiverr) list horn players at roughly US$50–300 per track; one subscription service (Musiversal) sold unlimited remote sessions including brass for about US$199 a month with the client owning the result. Low-medium confidence.
  - Swing bands in the dance community have funded albums by crowdfunding in the range of roughly US$10,000–40,000. Low confidence; no specific campaign verified.
  - Moral rights cannot be fully waived in several European countries, so the contract should record consent to the specific edits (looping, tempo change). High confidence in general terms.
- Not found: any published example from the swing-dance community of practice tracks commissioned at set tempos, of a band releasing dance-practice material under CC BY, or of what an Israeli or European band charges for a buy-out.
- Self-production with sampled instruments (the way the project's CC BY tracks were made) was not researched as to licence terms of sample libraries.

## 9. AI-generated music as a source of instrumental swing

### Takeaway
Verified for late 2026: Stability AI's terms give the user ownership of outputs "to the extent permitted by applicable law" and forbid passing them off as human-made; Google's terms say it "won't claim ownership"; several open models can be run locally with no service contract (ACE-Step 1.5 under MIT with BPM control). Not verified: the current terms of Suno and Udio and the state of their label deals, which decide whether downloads may be used in an app; and nothing was found on how good any of them is at big-band swing beyond the project's own negative experience with machine-made tracks.

### Cited Findings

**Open models that run locally**
- ACE-Step 1.5: "This project is licensed under [MIT]"; a "highly efficient open-source music foundation model" that "runs locally with less than 4GB of VRAM" in its small configuration; "Supports 10 seconds to 10 minutes (600s) audio generation"; metadata control of duration, BPM, key and time signature; XL variants released 2026-04-02 need 12–24 GB; the README warns of "unintentional copyright infringement due to stylistic similarity", asks users to "verify the originality of generated works" and to "clearly disclose AI involvement", and does not describe its training data. — [ACE-Step 1.5 README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)
- ACE-Step (first version, released 2025-05-06): "This project is licensed under Apache License 2.0"; generates instrumental tracks; "The authors are not responsible for any misuse of the model, including but not limited to copyright violations." — [ACE-Step README](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step)
- YuE (now "YuE2"): "Code, agent skill, and documentation: Apache 2.0. Model weights: CC BY-NC 4.0 with additional creator permission"; individuals may "monetize generated outputs, with no fees or royalties payable to us"; companies must ask for a commercial licence; needs a 24 GB GPU; instrumental generation from a description added September 2026. — [YuE README](https://github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE)
- DiffRhythm: "DiffRhythm (code and DiT weights) is released under the Apache License 2.0"; instrumental mode; up to 4 min 45 s; 8 GB of VRAM; warns of "unintentional copyright infringement through stylistic similarities". — [DiffRhythm README](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/DiffRhythm)

- Stable Audio Open is distributed through Stability's open tooling (code MIT) with weights on Hugging Face behind acceptance of separate terms. — [stable-audio-tools README](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-tools)

**Copyright status and labelling rules**
- US law protects works of "authors" for terms measured by the author's life (section 302, quoted in section 2). — [17 U.S.C. § 302, U.S. Code mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-states-law/blob/main/title-17/302.md)
- UK law has a specific rule for "computer-generated" literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works: the author is "the person by whom the arrangements necessary for the creation of the work are undertaken" (section 9(3)) and "copyright expires at the end of the period of 50 years from the end of the calendar year in which the work was made" (section 12(7)); the author of a sound recording is "the producer". — [CDPA 1988 s.9](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/9.md); [CDPA 1988 s.12, UK legislation mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/united-kingdom-law/blob/main/ukpga/1988/48/section/12.md)
- EU AI Act, Article 50(2): providers of systems generating synthetic audio must ensure that "outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated"; the deep-fake disclosure duty is lighter where content "forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or programme". Article 113 (consolidated text): "It shall apply from 2 August 2026", with listed exceptions that do not mention Article 50. — [AI Act Art. 50](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/ai-act/art-050.md); [AI Act Art. 113, EU law mirror](https://github.com/lexifina/european-union-law/blob/main/ai-act/art-113.md)

**Quality and reception: what the project itself recorded**
- "Avoid shipping AI-only tracks given the user's reaction to the Alex Morgan material"; the tracks of one Free Music Archive artist suspected to be "algorithmically generated" scored between 0 and 6 out of 10 with the user, against 7 for the real big band; "most swing uploads" on Pixabay "are labelled AI". — [project music notes, 2026-10-16/17](../../music/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Contract and copyright are separate questions.** A service's terms decide whether the developer may ship the output (ownership or licence, commercial use, download rights). Copyright decides whether anyone else can copy it and whether it copies someone else: output generated from a text prompt alone probably has no author in the US or EU sense, so it could not be protected against copying, and no service warrants that an output does not reproduce existing music (Stability's terms put that risk on the user).
- **For this app the realistic AI options are:** (1) a paid tier of a hosted service whose terms allow commercial use and downloads, checked on the day of use; (2) a local open model with a permissive licence on code and weights (ACE-Step 1.5 under MIT is the clearest; it also accepts a BPM target), which removes the service contract but leaves the training-data question and all responsibility for the output with the developer. Models with non-commercial weights (MusicGen; YuE2 for companies) carry the same NonCommercial doubt as the present band tracks.
- **Disclosure is the common thread** in the terms read (Stability, Google) and in the open models' READMEs: the app should say which tracks are machine-made rather than present them as band recordings; in the EU the marking duty of Article 50(2) falls on the provider of the generating system, not on an app that plays the files.
- **Fitness for dancing is unproven.** The only evidence in hand is the project's: the user rejected machine-made swing. Whether 2026 models produce a convincing horn section with stable tempo and real triplet swing would have to be tested with the project's own swing-ratio and beat-grid tools.

## 10. Comparison of the routes, and the most practical ones for one independent developer

### Takeaway
Three routes fit a free app by one developer: properly credited CC BY or CC0 tracks, a written permission from a small band (starting with the band already in the app), and commissioned recordings of public-domain or original tunes; per-track stock licences are a possible fourth once a licence tier that covers apps is confirmed. Swing-era recordings, business-to-business SDKs and collecting-society licensing do not fit a worldwide free app.

### Cited Findings
- The app needs the audio itself: it ships each track at seven tempos, plays its own metronome and voice over it, and times an animation to a per-track beat grid. — [project music notes](../../music/README.md)
- Distribution today is a public repository plus a public release of the APK; a store listing and a web app are intended. — [repository README](../../README.md); [app strings](../../app/src/main/res/values/strings.xml)
- The legal findings behind each row are in sections 1–9.

### Inferences
- **Comparison table** ("verified" = rests on Cited Findings above; "memory" = rests on unverified background):

- **Most practical, in order:**
  1. **Fix the credits now** (in-app credits screen with creator, source, licence link and change notice for all three tracks; licence version and link in the README; state that the tempo variants of the two band tracks are offered under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). This is required whatever else is decided, and curing it promptly restores the licence automatically.
  2. **Write to the band** for a one-page permission covering the app as it is (free, optional donations, tempo variants, synced animation, stores, public repository, demo videos), and ask who wrote the two tunes and whether the writers belong to a collecting society. Until there is an answer, treat the two tracks as unfit for a store release with an active donation link, and keep "Hot Swing" as the track that is safe everywhere.
  3. **Commission a small set of tracks** (for example three tunes at two tempos each) from a swing group, on free or original tunes, with a full transfer of rights, and release them under CC BY: the only route that yields real-band swing at chosen tempos with no licence questions left, and one that leaves the dance community something reusable.
  4. **Add a few purchased stock tracks** only after reading the specific licence for app use, copy limits and editing, and only if the audio can be kept out of the public repository.
  5. **Keep in reserve:** more CC BY material (it exists but is weak); a US-excluded build with pre-1963 European-free recordings of free tunes; machine-generated tracks from a permissively licensed local model or a paid service tier, labelled as such, if a listening test with the project's own swing measurements passes.
- **Not worth pursuing for this app:** business-to-business music services and society-plus-label licensing (built for funded services, and they do not hand over audio), and swing-era recordings for a worldwide release before the US dates of 2036 and later.
- Letting each user supply their own audio file is a further route that needs no licence from the developer; it is covered in the sibling notes on precedents.
