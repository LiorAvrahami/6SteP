# Precedents from similar products: how products that synchronise their own content with popular music get that music, and what happened to them (state as of November 2026)

*Research conditions (read before relying on these notes).* The session's shared web-search budget ran out roughly two-thirds of the way through, and direct page fetching was blocked for almost every host except github.com (developer.spotify.com, newsroom.spotify.com and techcrunch.com all failed to resolve). So: (1) most facts below come from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full page; quotes are as they appeared in those extracts; (2) GitHub pages (READMEs, issue threads, the DMCA repository) were read directly; (3) several planned look-ups were never run and are listed under "Gaps" in each section. Nothing in "Cited Findings" is from memory. Where I recall something relevant but could not source it, it is in "Gaps" and labelled *unverified recollection*.

## 1. Products built on Spotify (visualisers, lyrics sync, tempo-matching running apps, DJ apps, slow-downers, dance tools, quiz games): what each lost, and is any independent beat-synced Spotify web app still running in 2026?

### Takeaway
Every Spotify-based product in this class that changed tempo, mixed audio, or depended on Spotify's beat data has been cut off at least once (DJ apps July 2020; slow-downers September 2022; beat data for new apps 27 November 2024), and since 2025-26 an individual developer cannot get beyond a five-user, Premium-only "Development Mode". The only products that do these things on Spotify in 2026 are named commercial partners (rekordbox, Serato, djay since 24 September 2025; Philips Hue for lights). I found no confirmed independent beat-synced Spotify web app still working in 2026; the best-known one (Kaleidosync) is abandoned.

### Cited Findings

**Platform rule changes (timeline)**
- May 2015: libspotify deprecated; 16 May 2022: access disabled ("Sunset of libspotify on May 16, 2022"). Projects that broke: pyspotify (archived June 2022), Volumio's Spotify plugin, Mopidy/Iris setups, home-built players — [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2022-04-12-libspotify-sunset); [pyspotify](https://github.com/jodal/pyspotify); [Spotify community thread asking Spotify to reconsider](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Sunset-of-libspotify-Please-reconsider/td-p/5383105/highlight/true)
- 1 July 2020: Spotify ended access for all third-party DJ apps. Algoriddim (djay) said "no third-party DJ software will be able to access the Spotify library" after that date; a Spotify representative confirmed it to The Verge; no reason was given — [Engadget, 20 Mar 2020](https://www.engadget.com/2020/03/20/spotify-ends-support-third-party-dj-apps/); [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/2020/03/spotify-to-stop-working-in-djay/); [MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/spotify-will-soon-stop-working-with-third-party-dj-aps-says-algoriddim-as-djay-loses-support)
- Virtual DJ had already lost Spotify in 2017; Virtual DJ told DJ TechTools "Spotify is currently no longer available in VDJ 8" because of changed terms for professional DJ applications — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/spotify-to-end-access-for-all-dj-apps); [Input](https://www.inputmag.com/tech/spotify-to-discontinue-third-party-dj-app-support-in-july)
- 1 September 2022: Spotify withdrew the iOS/Android *streaming* SDKs that practice apps used to play (and slow down) Spotify audio. A commenter identifying as Roni Music's CTO quoted Spotify's notice that "important functionality in the streaming SDKs will stop working" after that date; users asked Spotify to keep them or to give the remaining "app remote" SDK the same capabilities — [Spotify community thread "Please continue supporting iOS/Android streaming SDKs"](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Please-continue-supporting-iOS-Android-streaming-SDKs/td-p/5416477/page/24)
- 27 November 2024: "Effective today, new Web API use cases will no longer be able to access or use the following endpoints": Related Artists, Recommendations, Audio Features, Audio Analysis, Featured Playlists (press also lists category playlists and 30-second preview URLs). "Applications with existing extended mode Web API access that were relying on these endpoints remain unaffected." Stated aim: "creating a more secure platform"; developers suspected it was aimed at AI scraping — [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2024-11-27-changes-to-the-web-api); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/27/spotify-cuts-developer-access-to-several-of-its-recommendation-features); [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/are-spotifys-changes-to-third-party-developer-access-meant-to-combat-ai-scraping)
- Effect on developers: audio-features and audio-analysis return 403 for apps without a quota extension granted or pending before 27 Nov 2024; one developer wrote that an app built over months "which relied deeply on the audio analysis ... now doesn't work" — [community thread on 403 errors](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Web-API-Get-Track-s-Audio-Features-403-error/m-p/6916386/highlight/true); ["Changes to Web API" thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Changes-to-Web-API/m-p/6541270/highlight/true)
- 15 May 2025: extended quota (the only way past the test-user cap) is accepted "only from organizations (not individuals)"; criteria include a legally registered business, a launched service and at least 250,000 monthly active users — [Spotify quota-modes documentation](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/quota-modes); [community thread on the new criteria](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Updating-the-Criteria-for-Web-API-Extended-Access/td-p/6920661/page/8)
- 6 February 2026 ("Update on Developer Access and Platform Security"): for Development Mode client IDs created from 11 February 2026, "Development Mode use will require a Spotify Premium account; Developers will be limited to one Development Mode Client ID; Each Client ID will be limited to up to five authorized users" (previously 25). Applied to existing apps from 9 March 2026. Spotify says Development Mode is for "learning, experimentation, and personal projects for non-commercial use by individual developers" and "should not be relied on as a foundation for building or scaling a business on Spotify"; reason given: risks from automation and AI. Spotify later postponed the *endpoint* cuts for existing apps but kept the Premium, five-user and one-client-ID limits — [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/amp/spotify-now-requires-premium-accounts-for-developer-mode-api-access/); [Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/devops/138608/spotify-puts-the-brakes-on-developer-mode-with-stricter-api-rules); [Spotify community update thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/February-2026-Spotify-for-Developers-update-thread/m-p/7330564/highlight/true)

**What Spotify's terms say about this class of product**
- Developer Policy (version effective 15 May 2025), prohibited applications: do not "synchronize any sound recordings with any visual media, including any advertising, film, television program, slideshow, video, or similar content" — [Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy/)
- Developer Terms: no device or system may "segue, mix, re-mix, or overlap any Spotify Content with any other audio content" — [Spotify Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms); quoted in [community thread on the no-mixing policy](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Question-regarding-no-mixing-policy/td-p/5282821)
- Spotify's compliance guide lists as not allowed: "syncing sound recordings accessed via the Spotify Platform with other recordings, lyrics, or video"; games or trivia quizzes ("name that tune"); and "any commercialization of streaming apps". It also states only Premium subscribers can stream through the platform — [Spotify compliance page](https://developer.spotify.com/policy/complying/); [compliance tips](https://developer.spotify.com/compliance-tips)
- Forum answers: a proposed app that would start and stop Spotify playback in time with embedded YouTube videos was told "No" (prohibited by two rules) — [community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Is-this-allowed-Small-Forum-Synchronizing-Music-To-Videos/td-p/6032312). Showing lyrics from another service in the same view as Spotify content was described as a "no-go" (a forum answer, not official policy) — [community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Prohibited-Applications-Questions/td-p/5491378). Games: "not allowed to use any kind of Spotify data in games or quizzes regardless of how it is made" — [community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/What-is-the-reason-for-not-allowing-games-and-quizzes/m-p/6219736/highlight/true)
- The direct question "Is making a visualizer allowed with the Spotify API?" (visuals generated from Web API song data) got no official answer in the thread — [community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Is-making-a-visualizer-allowed-with-the-Spotify-API/m-p/5616391/highlight/true); a related thread about a player over a looping background drew "I don't think it's allowed" — [community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Can-i-include-a-player-with-a-loop-background-behind/m-p/5661976/highlight/true)

**Visualisers**
- Kaleidosync (independent web visualiser, zachwinter): README says it began on The Echo Nest's pre-analysed data ("now part of Spotify's track analysis API"), version 6 "Leverages the Spotify Web Playback SDK", and the author later built "tooling for realtime audio analysis, right in the browser"; version 10 lists Spotify, Audius, Radio Paradise and KEXP as sources — [GitHub README](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync). Renamed "5HT (previously Kaleidosync)" in a May 2023 Reddit post — [Reddit mirror](https://nyc1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/spotify/comments/13ig3h8/introducing_5ht_previously_kaleidosync_a_webbased)
- Kaleidosync status, read directly from its issue tracker: "Spotify api wont link" (29 Feb 2024); "Website not loading" (18 Aug 2024); "This URL does not work" (15 Mar 2026); "The kaleidosync link leads to different websites each time you visit" (29 Apr 2026), with a user replying "Sadly, I think this project is abandoned"; 23 May 2026: the page loads but the Spotify button does nothing. No maintainer reply. A community fork (kaleidosync-react.vercel.app) appeared May-June 2026; whether its Spotify mode works is not stated — [issues list](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync/issues); [issue 107](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync/issues/107); [issue 105](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync/issues/105)
- A visualiser vendor's 2026 guide says that on 27 November 2024 Spotify stopped giving new Web API apps "the beat and section data many web visualizers used" (vendor source, treat with caution) — [freebeat.ai](https://freebeat.ai/articles/how-to-show-a-visualizer-while-playing-spotify-in-2026-desktop-and-phone-options)
- Official-partner counter-example: Philips Hue's Spotify integration syncs lights to the beat by analysing "the metadata of each track", so it "eliminates the need to use a microphone"; works with free and paid Spotify accounts; needs a Hue Bridge — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/philips-hue-and-spotify-are-partnering-to-make-your-smart-lights-party-worthy); [Trusted Reviews](https://www.trustedreviews.com/news/philips-hue-and-spotify-team-up-algorithmic-light-show-4161014); [TechHive](https://www.techhive.com/article/579648/philips-hue-smart-bulbs-can-now-sync-with-spotify.html)

**Practice slow-downers**
- Amazing Slow Downer (Roni Music) offered slowed, pitch-corrected Spotify playback via the streaming SDKs. A petition records that Spotify would "stop maintaining compatibility with Amazing Slow Downer from 1st September 2022"; the developer (Rolf Nilsson) later posted that "the combination of Amazing Slow Downer (ASD) and Spotify is now history". Users were pointed to Apple Music on iOS, where speed could be changed but not pitch — [Change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/keep-spotify-and-amazing-slow-downer-compatible); [Spotify community thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Please-continue-supporting-iOS-Android-streaming-SDKs/td-p/5416477/page/24)
- Anytune: its Android Spotify support came with "The waveform will not be accurate; pitch-shifting and analysis are not available. Spotify support is in development and subject to change. You must have Spotify Premium ... Songs streamed through Spotify are DRM-protected, limiting Anytune's functionality." Its current device-library article says Anytune "cannot currently play streamed music (i.e Apple Music, Spotify)" — [Anytune help: Android/Spotify](https://anytune.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360059477531); [Anytune help: device library](https://anytune.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015557892)

**DJ apps**
- Before July 2020: djay and others mixed Spotify tracks under Spotify's then terms; ended 1 July 2020 (above). Even then, djay disabled mix recording whenever Spotify tracks were loaded (user threads from the djay 2 era) — [Algoriddim community](https://community.algoriddim.com/t/recording-songs-from-spotify/5594)
- 24 September 2025: Spotify returned to DJ software, but only inside three named partners (rekordbox, Serato, djay), Premium only, 51 markets, desktop first; an 11 December 2025 update added rekordbox and djay on iOS/Android. Tracks cannot be stored offline and do not work with Serato Stems; Traktor and Virtual DJ were "left behind" — [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-24/dj-software-integration-premium); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/spotify-now-integrates-directly-with-dj-software-from-rekordbox-serato-and-djay); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/after-5-years-spotify-streaming-returns-to-dj-software-but-theres-a-catch); [DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2025/09/24/spotify-finally-slides-into-rekordbox-serato-and-algoriddim-djay-again-for-better-or-worse/)

**Tempo-matching running**
- Spotify's own Running feature (tempo matched to the runner, dating from 2015) was retired on 26 February 2018; users were sent to playlists and to Nike+ Run Club and Runkeeper integrations — [Spotify community announcement thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Content-Questions/Retirement-of-our-Running-Feature/td-p/4383603/page/6); [user petition](https://www.change.org/p/spotify-running-feature-from-2015)
- Third-party "BPM of this track" data now comes from non-Spotify analysis services (SoundStat, launched early 2025; Tunebat's paid API; Cyanite) or local analysis with Essentia; AcousticBrainz has been defunct since 2022 — [dev.to article](https://dev.to/birrings/spotifys-audiofeatures-api-died-in-2024-heres-what-i-built-to-replace-it-3dn3); ["Changes to Web API" thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Changes-to-Web-API/m-p/6541270/highlight/true)

**Quiz and party games**
- Hitster (commercial card game): each card's QR code is scanned by the Hitster app, which plays the track through the player's Spotify account; the FAQ offers a "30s preview mode" for Premium users with connection trouble, a Spotify Free mode, and 20 seconds per song without a Spotify account; some cards fail in some countries "due to how Spotify manages the music rights". The pages are undated — [Hitster FAQ](https://hitstergame.com/en-us/faq-v3/)
- Fan-made card generators use the Web API only for metadata and a QR code that "deep-links directly to the song on Spotify" — [article on a Hitster card generator](https://news.lavx.hu/article/bridging-digital-music-and-physical-play-the-hitster-card-generator)

**Unofficial clients (not using the official SDKs)**
- librespot (open-source reverse-engineered client library): "Using this code to connect to Spotify's API is probably forbidden by them. Use at your own risk." "librespot only works with Spotify Premium." Accounts created after a cutoff cannot obtain the audio decryption keys; "No workaround is currently known" — [librespot README](https://github.com/librespot-org/librespot)
- Mopidy-Spotify (2026 README): "Spotify has recently disabled streaming via third-party access tokens" and "Spotify have recently disabled username and password login for playback"; playback now relies on a librespot-based plugin; Premium only — [Mopidy-Spotify README](https://github.com/mopidy/mopidy-spotify)

### Inferences
- On Spotify, each of the three things the dance app needs is separately disfavoured in the written terms: visuals synced to recordings (Developer Policy), other audio overlapping Spotify audio (Developer Terms), and tempo change (no surviving route: the SDKs that exposed audio ended in 2022, and DJ tempo control exists only inside three partner apps).
- The products that still do these things in 2026 all have a commercial agreement with Spotify. The independent ones lost the capability without individual enforcement: Spotify simply withdrew the SDK, endpoint or quota tier.
- A solo developer's Spotify app in 2026 is capped at five authorised users (and the developer must hold Premium; streaming through the platform needs Premium anyway), which rules out a public free web app regardless of what it does.
- Kaleidosync's history suggests the pattern for independent visualisers: tolerated (never clearly approved) for years, then starved of data and abandoned, with realtime in-browser audio analysis as the author's escape route.

### Gaps
- No search was run for Spotify-based lyrics-sync products (for example Musixmatch's integration and its end), choreography tools, classroom tools, or individual running apps (RockMyRun, PaceDJ, jog.fm) that used Spotify data, so I cannot say what each lost.
- Whether Hitster operates under a Spotify partnership (given the policy bar on games) was not established.
- I could not confirm from Spotify's own text which additional endpoints the February 2026 change removed (one secondary report mentioned 15), nor the "thousands of apps shut down" claim in a [Headphonesty article](https://www.headphonesty.com/2026/02/spotify-crackdown-thousands-third-party-music-apps/), which names no apps in the extract I saw.
- No maintainer statement links Kaleidosync's abandonment to the API change; the cause is inferred from timing only.
- *Unverified recollection:* Heardle (bought by Spotify 2022, closed 2023) and Spotify-based "listen together" rooms such as JQBX were not checked.

## 2. Products built on YouTube embeds (Soundslice, Chordify, Ultimate Guitar, Songsterr, lyrics and karaoke sites, language overlays, dance-tutorial loopers, watch-together): how long have they run, do they also pay for licences, has YouTube acted against them?

### Takeaway
Putting your own synced content next to an unmodified, visible YouTube embedded player (and, in Soundslice's case, using the player's own speed control) is the longest-surviving pattern in this whole catalogue: Soundslice since 2012, Chordify since 2013, LyricsTraining since at least 2013, and I found no report of YouTube acting against any of them. Where these products also reproduce the *composition* (tabs, chords, lyrics) they separately license it from publishers. YouTube's enforcement has gone instead at products that strip the audio out of the player (Discord bots), wrap YouTube as an ad-free music service (Musi), or bypass the official player (Invidious, stream-rippers).

### Cited Findings

**Notation, chords and tabs synced to YouTube**
- Soundslice (Adrian Holovaty and PJ Macklin) launched in 2012 as a YouTube-based annotator; at first it only worked with YouTube videos. Users sync notation to a YouTube video or MP3 by tapping downbeats — [Holovaty's notes](https://holovaty.com/writing/soundslice); [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4790636); [Soundslice help: YouTube](https://www.soundslice.com/help/youtube); [Soundslice features](https://soundslice.com/features)
- Speed: Soundslice can only offer the speeds YouTube's player allows ("only certain speed values work for YouTube videos"); it announced when YouTube added 25% and 75%. Its "speed training" feature works with YouTube videos — [Soundslice help: playback speed](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/player/basic/5/playback-speed/); [blog: YouTube now supports 25 percent speed](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/67/youtube-now-supports-25-percent-speed); [blog: speed training](https://soundslice.com/blog/215/introducing-speed-training)
- Soundslice's legal position: its terms note that some content uses a YouTube embedded player and users agree to YouTube's terms; "Our customers are responsible for securing the rights to whatever music they're using Soundslice with"; selling sheet music needs a separate "print" licence; DMCA takedown process. In the 2012 launch thread the founder's line was that "YouTube is the host and already has well-formed copyright/takedown" processes — [Soundslice terms](https://www.soundslice.com/terms/); [licensing FAQ](https://www.soundslice.com/licensing/faq/); [store content policy](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/store-selling/policies/153/content-policy/); [HN comment](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4791225)
- Chordify launched in 2013 (founders Bas de Haas and Tijmen Ruizendaal), "transforms any song into chords"; its terms say the service is for "personal non-commercial use only" and give no warranty that use will not infringe third-party rights — [Chordify about](https://chordify.net/pages/about/); [Chordify terms](https://chordify.net/pages/terms-and-conditions/)
- Ultimate Guitar says it holds licences "with thousands of publishers and Artists" including Sony/ATV, EMI, Alfred, Hal Leonard, Peermusic, Faber and Music Sales, plus a Harry Fox Agency arrangement (Wikipedia dates it 10 April 2010); some songs are blocked for licensing reasons. This followed takedown demands from the MPA and NMPA against tab sites in the 2000s — [Ultimate Guitar licensing page](https://www.ultimate-guitar.com/article/blog/licensing); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_Guitar); [UG help](https://help.ultimate-guitar.com/en/articles/6735517-website-licensing-on-ultimate-guitar-why-are-some-songs-blocked); [law-journal paper](https://www.cardozoaelj.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/07/Myers.pdf)
- Songsterr sells "Pause-Free Sync with Original Audio" and pitch-preserving tempo change as paid features; a forum user reports the free tier syncs tabs with YouTube tracks — [Songsterr Plus](https://songsterr.com/plus); [Songsterr terms](https://www.songsterr.com/a/wa/terms); [BassBuzz forum](https://forum.bassbuzz.com/t/syncing-actual-songs-with-ultimate-guitar/57559)
- Other chord and learning services confirm that the composition licence is the fragile part: Yousician attributes missing songs to "licensing agreements with the publishers"; Flowkey removes songs when licences change; Jamzone says rights holders "can withdraw consent" even with publisher agreements in place; e-chords notes its BMI licence covers performance only, not reproduction — [Yousician support](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000758937-Why-can-t-I-find-a-popular-song-in-Yousician); [Flowkey help](https://help.flowkey.com/en/articles/8975623-why-are-some-songs-no-longer-available-on-flowkey); [Jamzone](https://jamzone.substack.com/p/pro-guide-recording-exporting-and/comments); [e-chords licences](https://www.e-chords.com/de/licenses)

**Lyrics, karaoke, rhythm games, language learning**
- LyricsTraining: a fill-in-the-gaps lyrics game that "embeds music videos from popular sites such as YouTube and Vevo"; documented in use since at least February 2013; teachers add their own YouTube links and lyrics — [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/lyricstraining); [2013 blog post](https://blogs.transparent.com/english/?p=2560); [TeachersFirst, Aug 2023](https://teachersfirst.org/blog/2023/08/tech-tool-of-the-month-lyricstraining-part-2/)
- AllKaraoke (independent, open source, "Online Karaoke game with pitch detection in your browser", 3,800+ commits): plays YouTube for the music; its README notes offline mode "Plays dummy local video instead of YouTube" and that "some songs won't work (YouTube will block the access)" — [GitHub](https://github.com/Asvarox/allkaraoke)
- Language Reactor (browser extension adding dual subtitles and vocabulary tools on YouTube and Netflix) is still distributed; a June 2025 support thread reports it stopped working on YouTube for some users — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_Reactor); [support forum](https://forum.languagelearningwithnetflix.com/latest)

**What YouTube's rules say**
- YouTube's policy guide accepts overlays for consent or "playback controls (e.g. mute, full screen, play, pause, etc.) ... so long as they do not conflict with the YouTube player UI elements"; a client must not use "overlays, frames or other visual elements to obscure any part of an embedded player", must not block standard player features, must keep the player at least 200 by 200 pixels, and must not autoplay until more than half the player is visible — [YouTube developer policies guide](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies-guide); [Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)
- YouTube does audit API clients: a forum-software vendor thread describes "YouTube API audits and ToS violations" over thumbnail overlays — [XenForo community](https://xenforo.com/community/threads/dealing-with-youtube-api-audits-and-tos-violations-linked-to-xfmg-thumbnail-overlays.206139)

**Where YouTube did act**
- Discord music bots: Groovy (16 million servers) received a cease-and-desist and shut on 30 August 2021; Rythm (20 million servers, 560 million users) shut on 15 September 2021: "Since we use YouTube as our main source of music ... our current service will shut down entirely." Both streamed the audio of YouTube videos into voice channels — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/09/13/youtube-groovy-bot-rythm-discord-letters/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2021/08/25/youtube-nukes-popular-groovy-bot-discord-music-service-for-breaking-platforms-tos/); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/a-discord-music-bot-with-560-million-users-is-being-taken-down)
- Rythm came back in June 2024 as a Discord activity with its own catalogue (about 50 million songs, a US$4.99 tier, limited solo listening); Discord separately tested an official YouTube "Watch Together" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/platforms/killed-by-google-in-2021-discords-most-popular-music-bot-is-back-from-the-dead); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/6/rythm-returns-popular-discord-music-bot-relaunches-as-activity-app-with-new-features); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210924-discord-watch-together-youtube-integration)
- Musi (free iOS music app built on YouTube streams): Apple removed it on 24 September 2024 after a complaint from "YouTube Legal" dated 29 July 2024; Musi sued Apple in October 2024 and lost, the judge holding Apple can remove apps "with or without cause" and (reported 18 March 2026) sanctioning Musi's lawyers. Labels and publishers had pressed for its removal for years — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/free-streaming-app-musi-sues-apple-over-removal-from-app-store/); [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/amp/apple-can-remove-apps-with-or-without-cause-judge-rules-in-musi-lawsuit/); [Complete Music Update, 18 Mar 2026](https://completemusicupdate.com/apple-wins-legal-battle-with-musi-after-judge-rules-music-apps-lawsuit-was-not-the-paradigm-of-candor/); [CMU, Oct 2024](https://completemusicupdate.com/musi-sues-apple-over-its-app-store-banishment/)
- Invidious (alternative YouTube front end that does not use the embed): on 8 June 2023 YouTube's legal team demanded it shut down, citing the API terms; the team replied publicly that "we don't use their API"; 14 July 2023: "YouTube didn't proceed further"; closed 7 November 2023 with "Nothing happened since" — [Invidious issue 3872](https://github.com/iv-org/invidious/issues/3872)

**Watch-together services**
- Watch2Gether-style rooms (YouTube, Vimeo, SoundCloud, no registration) and the open-source OpenTogetherTube synchronise playback of each viewer's own embedded player; Teleparty does the same for Netflix through a browser extension, with every viewer needing their own subscription (secondary sources) — [OpenTogetherTube README](https://github.com/dyc3/opentogethertube); [Watch2Gether overview](https://technicalustad.com/watch2gether-alternatives/); [Teleparty explainer](https://automatelife.net/is-it-safe-to-use-a-teleparty-netflix-party/)

### Inferences
- The line YouTube enforces is about the *player*, not about what sits beside it: keep the official player visible, unobscured and in control of playback (including ads), and synced third-party content next to it has gone unchallenged for over a decade. Products that take audio out of the player or replace it are the ones that received letters.
- Speed change on YouTube is a built-in, sanctioned player function, which is why Soundslice can offer it without any special arrangement; the trade-off is a fixed set of speeds.
- Licences paid by Ultimate Guitar, Yousician and similar are for reproducing the composition (tabs, chords, lyrics), not for the recording, which YouTube supplies. A product whose own content is original (for example a foot animation and a count) has no equivalent composition to license.
- Per-video failure is normal on this route: uploaders and labels can disable embedding or remove videos, as AllKaraoke's README acknowledges.

### Gaps
- Chordify's publisher licences: four differently phrased searches returned nothing about Chordify's licensing (results were swamped by an unrelated company, Chordal). I could not confirm that Chordify pays publishers in addition to using the embed.
- I found no evidence either way of YouTube acting against Soundslice, Chordify, Songsterr or LyricsTraining; absence of reports is not proof of approval.
- Not searched: dance-tutorial loop/slow/mirror sites built on YouTube, Riffstation's web player (the search only confirmed Fender's 2017 acquisition of its maker: [Fender newsroom](https://www.fender.com/newsroom/271778-fender-announces-acquisition-of-dublin-based-sonic-ladder/)), FluentU, YouTube-based karaoke apps (Yokee), and whether YouTube's terms say anything specific about playing separate audio (metronome, voice) alongside the player.
- Rhythm Plus (a community web rhythm game) is live on GitHub but its page does not state where the music comes from — [GitHub](https://github.com/henryzt/Rhythm-Plus-Music-Game).
- Whether each named product is still operating in November 2026 was confirmed only by live pages appearing in search results, not by a dated 2026 statement.

## 3. Products built on Apple Music (MusicKit web players and practice tools; the 2025 "DJ with Apple Music" partner integrations)

### Takeaway
Apple Music is the one consumer service where small independent apps have, for years, changed the speed of streamed subscription tracks (Perfect Tempo, Amazing Slow Downer) using Apple's own player, though without access to the audio itself (so pitch correction, EQ and analysis are limited) and with at least one outage caused by an iOS update. Mixing and DJ-grade tempo control arrived only through named partners in March 2025.

### Cited Findings
- "DJ with Apple Music" launched on 25 March 2025, built with AlphaTheta (rekordbox, OMNIS-DUO, XDJ-AZ), Serato, inMusic's Engine DJ, Denon DJ, Numark and RANE DJ; Algoriddim's djay had added Apple Music earlier. rekordbox 7.1.0 (Mac/Windows) and 4.4.0 (iOS) shipped that day — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/03/25/professional-djs-can-now-use-apple-music-tracks-directly); [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/tech/you-can-now-dj-apple-music-using-rekordbox-serato-and-more); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/rekordbox-adds-apple-music-support-alphatheta-update-software-dj-tech); [CDM](https://cdm.link/dj-with-apple-music/)
- Limits inside the partner apps: djay disables mix recording whenever a streaming service is in use (staff attribute this to "licensing agreements"), and "Neural Mix is not available for Spotify or Apple Music tracks" — [djay manual: streaming services](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/music-library/streaming-services); [djay manual: recording](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/mixing-basics/recording); [Algoriddim community](https://community.algoriddim.com/t/cant-record-mix-using-tracks-from-streaming-services/15400)
- Perfect Tempo (Open Planet Software, iOS, paid, marketed to "music, dance and fitness enthusiasts"): "speed up or slow down any song on your iPhone or Apple Music without affecting pitch"; with a subscription the user can play "any song from the entire Apple Music catalog"; 1% steps up to 50% faster or slower; section looping; setlists with a remembered tempo per song. Pitch controls are hidden by default because "many songs do not support being transposed". MacStories reviewed it as solving the problem that Spotify and Apple Music tracks "reintroduced DRM protection that didn't work with existing music tempo apps" — [developer site](https://openplanetsoftware.com/perfect-tempo); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/perfect-tempo/id592398757); [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/perfect-tempo-brings-tempo-control-to-apple-music-tracks/)
- Amazing Slow Downer (iOS) streams Apple Music, but "some functions such as 'Pitch change' and 'Equalizer' are not available when playing 'Apple Music' songs"; its listing records that speed change broke and "will work again when you update to iOS 15.5". Developers reported the same break: "playbackRate in MusicKit for Swift is non functional in iOS 15.4" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id308998718); [Apple developer forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/703048); [Apple developer forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/702397)
- Other practice apps describe the same wall: Transcribe+ says "due to DRM music streaming limitations, some features are unavailable on Apple Music tracks"; Anytune says "Apple has not made Apple Music streamed songs or songs saved for offline playback accessible to app developers" and does not play them at all — [Transcribe+ App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1048119179); [Anytune FAQ](https://develop.anytune.us/?p=5731)
- On the web: Apple's MusicKit JS reference shows a player-level playback-rate setter. A developer building a practice app on MusicKit JS reported audible hitches on seeks and rate changes; the reply was that sample-accurate audio manipulation "isn't part of either API surface". Another developer could only reach the underlying audio element through an internal, unsupported path — [MusicKit on the web reference](https://js-cdn.music.apple.com/musickit/v1/index.html); [Apple developer forum thread 829734](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/829734); [Apple developer forum thread 771715](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/771715)
- Independent MusicKit web players exist and are public: Musish, "The unofficial open source Apple Music webapp ... Built with React and Apple's MusicKit JS", signs users in on an Apple.com window; Cider's original open-source client was archived on 10 December 2024 in favour of a commercial version 2 — [Musish](https://github.com/Musish/Musish); [Cider](https://github.com/ciderapp/Cider)
- An official-partnership precedent for "our content synced to Apple Music playback": Fender's subscription app Fender Songs was announced as a partnership with Apple Music — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/fender-partners-with-apple-music-on-subscription-app-fender-songs/)

### Inferences
- Apple lets ordinary developers *control* playback of subscription tracks, including rate, but never hands over the audio. That is enough for "slow the song down while my own visuals follow", but not for analysis, pitch correction beyond what Apple's player does, or mixing in the DJ sense; those are what the 2025 partner programme licenses.
- Reliance on Apple's player has its own platform risk: one iOS point release silently broke tempo control for every such app until the next release.
- The web version (MusicKit JS) has the same rate control on paper, but the one developer report I found describes rough behaviour, so it should be tested before being relied on.

### Gaps
- I could not open Apple's own "DJ with Apple Music" page or MusicKit terms, so the statement that DJ integration is partner-only rests on press coverage naming the partners, not on Apple's wording.
- Not established: whether Perfect Tempo is still on sale under that name in November 2026 (one App Store result for the same app ID showed the name "Grooves: Song Speed Changer"), how it implements tempo change, and whether Musish's hosted site still works.
- Fender Songs' launch date, how its sync worked and whether it still exists were not retrieved (*unverified recollection:* launched 2019, later discontinued).
- No precedent found either way for playing extra audio (metronome, voice) over Apple Music playback.

## 4. DJ software after 2020: which streaming services license mixing and tempo change to third-party DJ apps, on what basis, and is any of it open to web apps or independent developers?

### Takeaway
Mixing and tempo change on streamed catalogue music is available only inside DJ products that have a commercial integration with each service, and the list of services per product changes by negotiation. I found no route by which a web app or an independent developer can obtain it, and the open-source DJ tools (Mixxx, LynDJ) are local-files only.

### Cited Findings
- Service coverage by product in 2025-26 (secondary round-ups): rekordbox: Apple Music, Beatport, Beatsource, TIDAL, SoundCloud, and Spotify from September 2025; Serato DJ: Apple Music, Beatport, Beatsource, SoundCloud, Spotify, TIDAL; Traktor: Beatport and Beatsource; VirtualDJ: TIDAL, SoundCloud, Beatport, Beatsource, Deezer; djay: Spotify, Apple Music, TIDAL, SoundCloud, Beatport, Beatsource; Engine DJ: Amazon Music, Apple Music, TIDAL, Beatport, Beatsource, SoundCloud — [DJ.Studio guide](https://dj.studio/blog/dj-software-integration); [Algoriddim help](https://help.algoriddim.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012493060-What-streaming-service-options-are-available-in-djay); [Serato streaming page](https://serato.com/dj/pro/streaming)
- TIDAL charges for it: "as of May 2024, TIDAL updated its subscription tier for all DJ apps", requiring the Individual plan plus a "DJ Extension" add-on — [Algoriddim help](https://help.algoriddim.com/hc/en-us/articles/360012493060-What-streaming-service-options-are-available-in-djay); [Algoriddim community](https://community.algoriddim.com/t/tidal-restrictions-clarification-with-5-15-update/26092)
- Conditions attached: no mix recording with streamed tracks; only Beatport and Beatsource allow offline storage inside djay; Spotify tracks cannot be stored offline or used with Serato Stems — [djay manual](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/mixing-basics/recording); [Algoriddim community](https://community.algoriddim.com/t/cant-record-mix-using-tracks-from-streaming-services/15400); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/after-5-years-spotify-streaming-returns-to-dj-software-but-theres-a-catch)
- Services have withdrawn and re-granted this at will: Spotify removed it from Virtual DJ in 2017 and from everyone on 1 July 2020, then restored it for three partners on 24 September 2025 — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2020/03/20/spotify-ends-support-third-party-dj-apps/); [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-24/dj-software-integration-premium)
- Open-source DJ software: Mixxx's FAQ lists only local file formats and has no entry for any streaming service; LynDJ (for Lindy Hop socials) plays WAV, MP3, FLAC and OGG files from the DJ's own library — [Mixxx FAQ](https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/wiki/Faq); [LynDJ](https://github.com/Ghostkeeper/LynDJ)

### Inferences
- Tempo change plus overlapping audio is exactly what consumer-service developer terms forbid by default (Spotify's "segue, mix, re-mix, or overlap" clause) and what DJ partner deals carve out. A beat-synced practice app with a metronome and voice over the music is, in licensing terms, closer to a DJ app than to a player.
- Because these are bilateral deals, capability can vanish for an entire product category on one date (July 2020) and the smaller vendors (Traktor, Virtual DJ in 2025) can be left out when it returns.

### Gaps
- The search for web-based DJ products (You.DJ, Beatport's browser DJ app, DJ.Studio's YouTube mode) and for any public developer programme at TIDAL, Beatport, SoundCloud or Deezer was not run because the search budget was exhausted. "Not available to independent developers" is therefore an inference from the absence of any such product in the round-ups, not a sourced statement.
- The current price of TIDAL's DJ Extension and the 2026 status of Deezer and Amazon Music integrations were not confirmed.

## 5. Products that use the user's own files (slow-downers, transcription tools, rhythm games with user-supplied songs, local-library DJ apps)

### Takeaway
"Bring your own audio file" is the stable, unrestricted route: the app gets the real audio, so tempo, pitch, analysis and overlaid audio all work, and none of the practice tools on this route has been cut off. The cost is that DRM-protected streaming tracks are excluded, and that communities which *share* the audio alongside charts live with takedowns.

### Cited Findings
- Anytune "only plays files stored on the device that aren't DRM-protected" — [Anytune help](https://anytune.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015557892)
- Riffstation Pro (desktop) "will continue to allow users to upload any digital audio file from their personal music library" — [Fender newsroom, 2017](https://www.fender.com/newsroom/271778-fender-announces-acquisition-of-dublin-based-sonic-ladder/)
- Soundslice accepts an uploaded MP3 as well as a YouTube link, and offers finer speed control on non-YouTube audio — [Soundslice blog](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/50/new-speed-changing-interface-organization-accounts-and-more/)
- Dance-teacher players work the same way: Tempo SlowMo imports tracks from the device; Cadance changes "the tempo of eligible local music without changing its pitch" (a higher paid tier adds "DRM-protected music and playback of downloaded Apple Music tracks"); DanceTime Deluxe imports music from the device library and adjusts its speed — [Tempo SlowMo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id460008289); [Cadance](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6748429968); [DanceTime Deluxe](https://apps.apple.com/app/dancetime-deluxe/id403053954)
- Rhythm games with community songs:
  - osu!: uploaders need permission; beatmaps "risk removal" otherwise; the Featured Artist programme is a library of songs cleared for use; lead developer peppy said in March 2023 that when a rights holder wants content removed osu! should respect that; a 2014 Konami claim removed ranked maps from the download server — [osu! content usage guidelines](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Usage_permissions); [peppy on Reddit, mirror](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/osugame/comments/11j5db2/thoughts_on_recent_dmcarelated_discussions/jb39s8s/?context=3); [The Vault, 2021](https://thevaultpublication.com/2021/03/04/free-mp3s-or-free-promotion-the-issue-of-music-copyright-in-community-rhythm-games)
  - Beat Saber: custom songs are unofficial mods; a guide states their use on Quest "violates these terms of use"; the community host BeatSaver runs a DMCA process; the studio has said most custom songs are unlicensed uses. Meta's March 2026 DMCA action was against a game-piracy group, not the custom-song sites — [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/beat-saber-custom-song-guide-installation-and-downloads/); [BeatSaver DMCA policy](https://beatsaver.com/policy/dmca); [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/meta-shut-down-vrpirates-pirated-quest-games/)
  - YARG (open-source Rock Band-style game): "YARG itself does not use any ripped/pirated assets or music and never will"; users import content; an optional official setlist is offered — [YARG README](https://github.com/YARC-Official/YARG)
  - Other community games publish block lists and DMCA policies: 7th Beat Games keeps a list of "Songs that cannot be used even with permission"; Cytoid has a copyright policy — [7th Beat](https://7thbeat.notion.site/Songs-that-cannot-be-used-even-with-permission-e8fdb371dced4c6eb9f15e30a2b9140d); [Cytoid](https://cytoid.io/pages/dmca)

### Inferences
- The split that matters is whether the product *distributes* audio. Tools where each user supplies a file they already have (Anytune, Amazing Slow Downer, Transcribe!, LynDJ) have no recorded enforcement; communities that bundle the audio with the chart (osu!, BeatSaver) run takedown processes and lose songs.
- A chart or timing file that contains no audio and is matched to the user's own copy is the low-risk version of "user-distributed content".

### Gaps
- Not searched: Moises (terms on uploaded music, label relationships), Transcribe! and Audipo statements on streaming, Clone Hero and StepMania community practice, Audiosurf and Beat Hazard (which accept user files and, in later versions, other sources). I can say nothing sourced about these.
- I did not find how many users of practice tools still own audio files in 2026, which decides how useful this route is.

## 6. Products with licensed catalogues (dance and fitness apps, tempo-adaptive running apps, and the business-to-business providers they use): cost, and can small apps use it?

### Takeaway
Shipping famous recordings inside the product means direct licences from labels and publishers or a business-to-business provider such as Feed.fm or Tuned Global, priced by quote, built for funded companies, and still not permanent: catalogues shrink when licences lapse or the owner changes (Supernatural in 2026). Peloton shows the cost of getting it wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Peloton: members of the National Music Publishers' Association sued in 2019 over unlicensed songs in class videos; the claim rose from US$150 million to more than US$300 million (Variety: 2,468 songs; other outlets US$370 million); Peloton's antitrust counterclaim was dismissed in January 2020; the parties settled on 27 February 2020 with a "joint collaboration agreement", terms undisclosed — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/music-publishers-and-peloton-reach-settlement-over-copyright-infringement-lawsuit); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr-esq/peloton-nmpa-announce-settlement-agreement-dismiss-copyright-infringement-lawsuit-1281549); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2020/02/27/peloton-settles-music-licensing-lawsuit/); [Variety via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/peloton-settles-legal-fight-music-144605187.html)
- Feed.fm (music for fitness and other apps): "a monthly subscription service with fees based on variables including: level of curation customization, how many song streams you plan to do in a month, and what use case you need covered"; pricing is by request; an aggregator lists "from $250" (unverified). Feed.fm stresses that ordinary performing-rights licences "do not include the right to use the songs in fitness classes" — [Feed.fm licensing 101](https://www.feed.fm/music-licensing-101); [Feed.fm pricing page](https://www.feed.fm/pricing-page); [FitGap listing](https://us.fitgap.com/products/feed-fm); [Feed.fm blog](https://blog.feed.fm/music-licensing-for-fitness)
- Steezy (online dance classes): a vendor case study says its instructors search a cleared catalogue and teach to full commercial tracks through Tuned Global (vendor's own account) — [CB Insights profile carrying the case study](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/steezy); Steezy's own explainer on [music copyright for dancers](https://www.steezy.co/posts/music-copyright-for-dancers)
- Supernatural (VR fitness to licensed hits): Meta ended new content in January 2026; the product was spun out to its founders and relaunched on 21 October 2026 at about US$20 a month (double Meta's price); its FAQ promises only "as much of the original library as licensing allows" — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/supernatural-to-return-under-new-independent-company-as-coaches-rejoin-platform/); [9to5Google, 21 Oct 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/21/supernatural-the-best-vr-workout-app-makes-its-glorious-comeback-today/); [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/?p=33196)
- Licensed catalogues lose songs: Singa and KaraFun (karaoke), Yousician and Flowkey (learning) all tell users that songs disappear when rights holders or licence terms change — [Singa help](https://help.singa.com/en/articles/15516950-why-are-some-songs-or-artists-not-available-on-singa); [KaraFun help](https://www.karafun.com/en-au/help/catalog_83.html); [Flowkey help](https://help.flowkey.com/en/articles/8975623-why-are-some-songs-no-longer-available-on-flowkey)
- Publishers themselves name fitness/health, social and gaming as the main start-up categories seeking licences (January 2023) — [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2023/01/20/music-publishers-talk-innovation/)
- For in-person classes, studios buy blanket licences (PPL PRS in the UK, OneMusic in Australia, SESAC/ASCAP/BMI in the US); these do not cover an app — [PPL PRS](https://pplprs.co.uk/themusiclicence/sectors/fitness-dance/fitness-exercise-and-dance-classes/); [OneMusic](https://onemusic.com.au/licences/dance-schools/); [Dance Teacher](https://dance-teacher.com/dance-studio-music-licensing/)

### Inferences
- A free app by one developer has no revenue to set against a quote-based monthly fee plus per-stream charges, so this route is realistic only with re-recorded, royalty-free or directly licensed small-label music, not famous recordings.
- Even paying products do not get permanence; the catalogue is a lease.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for what Feed.fm, Tuned Global or direct label deals actually cost a small app; Feed.fm's own "what does it cost" article was not retrievable.
- Not searched or not confirmed: Just Dance, Zumba, Les Mills, Aaptiv, RockMyRun and Weav Run (the only Weav Run facts found were a 2015 Product Hunt launch and a 2021 review — [Food & Nutrition](https://foodandnutrition.org/from-the-magazine/weav-run-version-ios-4-1-1/)). Their licensing models and 2026 status are unknown to these notes.

## 7. Products in the swing, Lindy Hop, ballroom, salsa and tango world: how do they source music, and do any sync visuals to recordings?

## 8. Products that listen through the microphone to follow music: who does it, and how well does it work according to users?

### Takeaway
Two different things are done by listening. Estimating tempo from a live microphone works but lags by a second or more and assumes simple metre. Recognising a known recording and then locking pre-made timed content to it (Shazam's synced lyrics) has shipped to a mass audience since 2011 and is the stronger precedent for a follow-along mode.

### Cited Findings
- liveBPM (iOS beat detector): the developer claims accuracy "within 0.1 BPM under certain conditions" and suggests clapping or an external microphone; a drummer reports a delay of "about one to three seconds" and that it assumes 4/4; others call it "very very close"; ratings around 3.7 to 4.0 from a handful of reviews — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-beat-detector/id554766778); [review aggregator](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/livebpm-beat-detector/554766778?country=us)
- Shazam: after identifying a song through the microphone it shows time-synced lyrics that follow the music ("available only with a newly identified song"; not all songs have lyrics). The feature began as LyricPlay in 2011, after Shazam bought a synchronised-lyrics company, and was rebuilt as real-time lyric syncing in February 2018 — [Apple support](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/shazam/dev3e403546e/web); [Shazam press release, 2011](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/us/shazam/pressreleases/shazam-makes-first-corporate-acquisition-with-synchronized-lyrics-890839); [9to5Mac, 2018](https://9to5mac.com/2018/02/15/shazam-update-real-time-lyrics-syncing/)
- Smart lights: before the 2021 Spotify integration, Hue music sync worked through the phone's microphone; reviewers presented the metadata-based integration as the improvement because it "can anticipate the beat" instead of reacting to it — [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/home/your-hue-lights-can-now-sync-directly-with-spotify); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/philips-hue-and-spotify-are-partnering-to-make-your-smart-lights-party-worthy)
- Visualisers: Kaleidosync's author moved from Spotify's pre-computed analysis to "realtime audio analysis, right in the browser" — [Kaleidosync README](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync)

### Inferences
- Reactive beat detection is always late and cannot know where the bar or phrase starts; a product with its own beat map only needs the microphone to answer "which song, and where in it are we", which is the Shazam pattern.
- Listening sidesteps every streaming service's developer terms because the app never touches the stream, but adds a microphone permission and depends on room acoustics and speaker volume.

### Gaps
- Searches for user opinion on microphone-driven light sync (Nanoleaf, Govee, Hue Sync), beat-following metronomes, and listen-based lighting controllers were either not run or returned only unreliable content-farm pages; no trustworthy latency measurements were found.
- No precedent found for a web app that re-synchronises continuously to a recording through the browser microphone.

## 9. Enforcement precedents relevant to the "virtual browser" idea (Discord music bots, stream-ripping, watch-party virtual browsers, products that automate a consumer account)

### Takeaway
Services that sat between users and a consumer streaming service and re-delivered its audio have been shut by legal letter or lawsuit (Groovy, Rythm, Musi, stream-rippers), and the services keep closing the technical doors too (Spotify disabled third-party token streaming and password log-in for playback). Hosted virtual browsers have survived mainly for non-DRM content or as self-hosted software; I found nothing that makes a server-side browser logged in to a user's music subscription look safe.

### Cited Findings
- Groovy and Rythm (2021) and Musi (2024-26): see section 2 — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/09/13/youtube-groovy-bot-rythm-discord-letters/); [Complete Music Update](https://completemusicupdate.com/apple-wins-legal-battle-with-musi-after-judge-rules-music-apps-lawsuit-was-not-the-paradigm-of-candor/)
- Stream-ripping: the operator of FLVTO and 2conv was ordered to pay about US$83 million and abandoned his appeal in 2023; Yout's suit against the RIAA was dismissed at district level and appealed to the Second Circuit (outcome not found) — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/labels-awarded-83m-in-lawsuit-against-operator-of-stream-ripping-sites1234); [TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/youtube-rippers-run-out-of-money-give-up-on-83m-riaa-legal-battle-230810/); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/connecticut/ctdce/3:2020cv01602/141714/61/)
- youtube-dl: on 23 October 2020 the RIAA sent GitHub a notice arguing the code's "clear purpose" was to "circumvent the technological protection measures" (YouTube's "rolling cipher") under the anti-circumvention law — [GitHub DMCA repository](https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2020/10/2020-10-23-RIAA.md)
- Automated use of Spotify: Spotify and the three major labels sued Anna's Archive over an automated scrape of about 86 million audio files; preliminary injunction 20 January 2026; default judgment of US$322.2 million and a permanent injunction in April 2026 — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/record-labels-spotify-sue-pirate-group-annas-archive-over-scraping-and-brazen-theft-of-86m-tracks); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/annas-archive-to-pay-322million-after-losing-court-case-for-scraping-nearly-all-of-the-worlds-commercial-sound-recordings-from-spotify-3940673)
- Spotify also clamped down on modified clients in 2025 and, per open-source client maintainers, "disabled streaming via third-party access tokens" and "username and password login for playback" — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/spotify-clamps-down-on-modded-apps/); [Mopidy-Spotify README](https://github.com/mopidy/mopidy-spotify)
- Virtual browsers: Rabb.it (shared cloud browser, used heavily for Netflix) closed on 31 July 2019; one account says investors withdrew and "legality played no part"; Kast bought its assets. Hyperbeam offers a hosted shared browser; a 2026 guide warns that log-ins are "processed within a third-party browser environment, and DRM can still block playback". neko is a self-hosted successor ("watch party" is a stated use) whose README is silent on DRM and terms — [Wikipedia mirror](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Rabb.it); [TechJunkie](https://www.techjunkie.com/?p=173642); [Hyperbeam](https://watch.hyperbeam.com/blog/What-is-Hyperbeam/); [watch-party guide](https://arjankc.com.np/blog/online-group-movie-watching-guide); [neko](https://github.com/m1k1o/neko)
- Recording on the user's behalf: PlayOn Cloud has recorded Netflix, Hulu and others from a cloud machine since 2016, argues fair use under the Betamax case, says it "has ... not faced any legal challenges from video providers", and is described as screen capture rather than DRM circumvention; commentators note users may still breach the services' terms — [TechCrunch, 2016](https://techcrunch.com/2016/11/11/playon-cloud-lets-you-record-and-download-videos-from-streaming-services-to-your-iphone); [StreamTV Insider](https://www.streamtvinsider.com/online-video/playon-cloud-offers-mobile-offline-viewing-for-netflix-hulu-and-more); [Consumerist](https://consumerist.com/2015/11/06/you-can-record-movies-off-netflix-or-music-off-spotify-but-youre-not-allowed-to/)
- The tolerated contrast: tools that only synchronise each participant's *own* official player and subscription (Teleparty, Watch2Gether, OpenTogetherTube) continue to operate — [OpenTogetherTube](https://github.com/dyc3/opentogethertube); [Teleparty explainer](https://automatelife.net/is-it-safe-to-use-a-teleparty-netflix-party/)

### Inferences
- The enforcement pattern is consistent: re-transmitting or extracting a service's audio from infrastructure you run draws action once the product is visible; coordinating the user's own official client does not.
- A server-side browser playing a user's Spotify or Apple Music account would combine the features of the cases that ended badly (the developer's infrastructure handles credentials and re-streams audio) and would also have to defeat DRM and automated-use detection that the services are actively tightening.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: how Netflix, Spotify or Apple technically or legally responded to hosted virtual browsers; any case specifically about a service logging in to users' *music* accounts on a server; the Aereo and VidAngel rulings (*unverified recollection:* both went against services that acted as the user's remote proxy); the youtube-dl reinstatement (*unverified recollection:* GitHub restored it in November 2020).

## 10. General lessons commentators and developers draw about building a music-dependent product on a consumer streaming service's API ("platform risk")

### Takeaway
The platforms now say it themselves: the free developer tier is not a foundation for a product. Developers who built on it describe a gap they cannot cross (five users to 250,000), and the historical record is that access is withdrawn by category and by date, without individual fault.

### Cited Findings
- Spotify, February 2026: Development Mode "should not be relied on as a foundation for building or scaling a business on Spotify" — [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2026-02-06-update-on-developer-access-and-platform-security)
- Developers on the gap: "Development Mode is limited to 5 users, while Extended Quota seems to require organizations with ~250k MAUs, which creates a huge gap"; "it is obviously plain impossible to magically go from 25 users to 250.000 users to even apply for extended quota" — [Spotify community, Feb 2026 thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/February-2026-Spotify-for-Developers-update-thread/m-p/7368381); [Spotify community, extended-access thread](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Updating-the-Criteria-for-Web-API-Extended-Access/td-p/6920661/page/8)
- Lee Martin (independent developer of artist campaign sites) in "The State of Spotify Web API Report 2025": under the May 2025 criteria he "would no longer be able to build Spotify Web API powered artist campaigns which require user authentication" — [dev.to](https://dev.to/leemartin/the-state-of-spotify-web-api-report-2025-4gh3)
- Earlier cycles of the same thing: Spotify closed its in-client app platform to new submissions in 2014; ended libspotify (2022), DJ access (2020) and the streaming SDKs (2022) — [TechCrunch, 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/03/24/spotify-shutters-app-submissions); [Spotify blog](https://developer.spotify.com/blog/2022-04-12-libspotify-sunset)
- The developer of Amazing Slow Downer, after years of paid Spotify integration: "the combination of Amazing Slow Downer (ASD) and Spotify is now history" — [Spotify community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Please-continue-supporting-iOS-Android-streaming-SDKs/td-p/5416477/page/24)
- Distribution platforms are a second point of failure: the Musi ruling confirmed Apple may remove an app "with or without cause" following a third party's complaint — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/amp/apple-can-remove-apps-with-or-without-cause-judge-rules-in-musi-lawsuit/)
- Licensed music is also revocable: rights holders "can withdraw consent" (Jamzone); Supernatural's relaunch keeps only what "licensing allows" — [Jamzone](https://jamzone.substack.com/p/pro-guide-recording-exporting-and/comments); [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/supernatural-to-return-under-new-independent-company-as-coaches-rejoin-platform/)

### Inferences
- The durable designs keep the music source replaceable: the product's own value (beat maps, animation, count, voice) is stored independently of any service, and the player is a thin adapter that can be swapped (YouTube embed, Apple Music, local file, microphone).
- Risk is lowest where the feature used is one the platform offers to everyone as a consumer feature (YouTube's embed and its speed menu) and highest where it depends on a developer-only data endpoint or SDK.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Hacker News threads or independent post-mortems on the 2024-26 Spotify changes; the developer voices above come from Spotify's own forum and one dev.to report.

## 11. Cross-cutting patterns: which combinations of "sync to visuals", "tempo change" and "extra audio on top" have survived for years on which services, and which were cut off?

### Takeaway
Synced visuals plus built-in speed control beside the official YouTube player has survived 12 to 14 years for independent products; speed control of Apple Music through Apple's player has survived for independent iOS apps with limits; on Spotify every one of the three capabilities has been cut off for independents and exists only for partners. Extra audio over the stream is licensed only in DJ partner deals, and I found no independent product that both streams from a consumer service and overlays its own audio as a stated feature.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube embed, synced visuals, player-provided speed: Soundslice since 2012, Chordify since 2013, LyricsTraining since at least 2013, AllKaraoke; no enforcement found; rule is not to obscure or alter the player — [Holovaty's notes](https://holovaty.com/writing/soundslice); [Chordify about](https://chordify.net/pages/about/); [YouTube Required Minimum Functionality](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/required-minimum-functionality)
- YouTube, audio taken out of the player: cut off by legal letter (Groovy, Rythm 2021; Musi 2024) — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/09/13/youtube-groovy-bot-rythm-discord-letters/)
- Spotify, synced visuals: prohibited in the Developer Policy; independent visualiser tolerated for years then starved of beat data (27 Nov 2024) and abandoned (2026); partner example Philips Hue — [Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy/); [Kaleidosync issue 107](https://github.com/zachwinter/kaleidosync/issues/107); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/philips-hue-and-spotify-are-partnering-to-make-your-smart-lights-party-worthy)
- Spotify, tempo change: independent slow-downers ended 1 Sept 2022; DJ apps ended 1 July 2020, restored 24 Sept 2025 for three partners only — [Spotify community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Please-continue-supporting-iOS-Android-streaming-SDKs/td-p/5416477/page/24); [Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-09-24/dj-software-integration-premium)
- Spotify, extra audio on top: Developer Terms bar overlapping Spotify content with other audio; DJ partners are the exception — [Spotify Developer Terms](https://developer.spotify.com/terms)
- Apple Music, tempo change: independent apps (Perfect Tempo; Amazing Slow Downer) change speed of streamed tracks via Apple's player; pitch, EQ and analysis restricted; broke in iOS 15.4, fixed in 15.5 — [Perfect Tempo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/perfect-tempo/id592398757); [Amazing Slow Downer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id308998718)
- Apple Music and the DJ services, mixing: partners only, with recording disabled — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/03/25/professional-djs-can-now-use-apple-music-tracks-directly); [djay manual](https://help.algoriddim.com/user-manual/djay-pro-mac/mixing-basics/recording)
- User's own files: all three capabilities, no cut-offs recorded for single-user tools — [Anytune help](https://anytune.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015557892); [LynDJ](https://github.com/Ghostkeeper/LynDJ)
- Microphone recognition plus synced content: Shazam lyrics since 2011 — [Shazam press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/us/shazam/pressreleases/shazam-makes-first-corporate-acquisition-with-synchronized-lyrics-890839)

### Inferences
Summary matrix (my synthesis of the findings above; "independent" means no commercial deal with the service):

| Music source | Visuals synced to the recording | Tempo change | Product's own audio over the music | Track record for independents |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube embedded player | Survived 12-14 years beside an unobscured player | Yes, the player's fixed speed steps | No precedent found; no rule found either way | Longest-lived; per-video embed blocks are the main failure |
| Spotify (Web API, Web Playback SDK) | Against written policy; tolerated, then data removed 2024 | None since 2022 for independents | Against written terms | Cut off repeatedly; five-user cap since 2026 |
| Apple Music (Apple's player, MusicKit) | One partnership (Fender Songs); no independent example found | Yes on iOS via Apple's player; web behaviour reported rough | No precedent found | Works with limits; exposed to OS changes |
| DJ partner integrations (TIDAL, Beatport, Beatsource, SoundCloud, Apple, Spotify) | Not the use case | Yes | Yes (mixing), recording disabled | Not open to independents as far as found |
| User's own files | Yes | Yes, with pitch correction | Yes | Stable; excludes DRM streaming tracks |
| Microphone (recognise and follow) | Yes (Shazam lyrics) | Not applicable (music plays elsewhere) | Not applicable (separate device or speaker) | Stable; quality depends on acoustics |
| Licensed catalogue | Yes | Depends on licence | Depends on licence | Quote-priced; catalogue can shrink |

- The combination the dance app wants (visuals + tempo + metronome and voice over a famous recording) has a long independent track record only on user-supplied files. On YouTube the first two have precedent and the third is untested. On Apple Music the second has precedent on iOS. On Spotify none is open to an independent developer in 2026.

### Gaps
- The "extra audio on top" column is the weakest: I found written rules only for Spotify, and no product on YouTube or Apple Music that advertises its own metronome or voice over streamed music, so tolerance there is unknown rather than established.
- Because the search budget ran out, several products named in the brief (Moises, Transcribe!, Audipo, Clone Hero, StepMania, Just Dance, Zumba, Les Mills, Aaptiv, RockMyRun, web DJ apps, YouTube dance-loop sites) are missing from the matrix's evidence base.
